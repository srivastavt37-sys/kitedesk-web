GST Credit Notes, Cancelled Order Refunds & Sequential Invoice Numbering Rules in India

November 19, 2026 • 10 min read • 2034 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

A customer orders a ₹18,000 custom leather desk setup on Tuesday morning, receives your PDF tax invoice INV-2026-104 with your GSTIN and UPI QR code, and cancels the order on Wednesday afternoon before the courier picks up the parcel. Or a D2C shopper buys a ₹1,499 pair of handcrafted jootis, refuses the COD delivery at the doorstep, and asks for a replacement or refund.

Most small Indian sellers make one of two expensive mistakes right here. Either they open their billing folder, hit Delete on INV-2026-104, and reuse that same number for the next buyer—or they refund the full ₹18,000 via UPI and forget to reverse the ₹2,745.76 in output GST they already booked. Both habits cost real money. Deleting an issued invoice breaks your consecutive serial sequence under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules and leaves a gap in GSTR-1 Table 13. Refunding without a Credit Note leaves you paying ₹2,746+ in GST to the government out of your own pocket on a sale that never happened.

This guide breaks down the exact Section 34 Credit Note workflow, the ₹1,499 and ₹18,000 refund math, and how to keep your invoice numbering airtight using BillFold without triggering GSTR-1 audit notices.

Why Deleting an Issued GST Invoice Causes GSTR-1 Audit Mismatches

Under Rule 46(b) of the CGST Rules, 2017, every tax invoice must carry a consecutive serial number not exceeding 16 characters, unique for a financial year (April 1 to March 31). You can use letters, digits, hyphens, or slashes—such as INV-2026-104 or KD/26-27/104—but once an invoice is issued and shared with a customer, that number is permanently consumed.

When you file GSTR-1, Table 13 (Documents Issued) forces you to declare the exact serial range for the month: for example, From: INV-2026-081, To: INV-2026-120, Total Issued: 40, Cancelled: 1, Net Issued: 39. Notice how the GST portal tracks cancelled documents explicitly as a count rather than letting you pretend the number never existed.

  • Missing sequence red flags: If your Table 13 says 081 to 120 (40 numbers) and you only report 39 B2B/B2C invoices with 0 cancelled, automated GST scrutiny flags the gap. During a departmental audit or ASMT-10 notice, you have to produce the physical or digital copy marked CANCELLED.
  • Duplicate number collisions: If you reuse INV-2026-104 for a different buyer after deleting the first PDF, the original buyer (especially a B2B company claiming Input Tax Credit) may already have your first PDF in their accounting ledger. Two different taxable values under the same invoice number and GSTIN cause immediate GSTR-2B reconciliation failures.
  • UPI transaction reference mismatches: When your invoice embeds a dynamic UPI QR code, the tr= (Transaction Reference) parameter ties the bank settlement directly to INV-2026-104. Reusing that invoice number mixes two different customer payments under one reference ID in your bank statement.

The Exact Math: ₹1,499 D2C Order vs. ₹18,000 Cancelled Order

Let us run the numbers on two real Indian e-commerce scenarios so you can see how much output GST you lose if you skip issuing a Credit Note.

Scenario A: ₹1,499 Apparel or Footwear Cancellation (12% and 18% GST)

Suppose you sell a pair of ethnic jootis or a cotton kurta set for ₹1,499 (GST-inclusive MRP) and the buyer cancels or returns the item for a full refund.

  • At 12% GST (6% CGST + 6% SGST, or 12% IGST): Your taxable value is ₹1,499 ÷ 1.12 = ₹1,338.39. Your output GST liability is ₹1,499 − ₹1,338.39 = ₹160.61 (₹80.30 CGST + ₹80.31 SGST).
  • At 18% GST (9% CGST + 9% SGST, or 18% IGST): Your taxable value is ₹1,499 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,270.34. Your output GST liability is ₹1,499 − ₹1,270.34 = ₹228.66 (₹114.33 CGST + ₹114.33 SGST).

If 12 customers cancel ₹1,499 orders (18% slab) in a single festive month and you only refund them via UPI without filing Credit Notes, you hand over 12 × ₹228.66 = ₹2,743.92 in unrecovered GST to the tax department—on top of losing forward and reverse courier fees. Before you set your retail MRP or refund policy, run your SKU numbers through MarginMint to see how a 15% RTO or cancellation rate eats into your net contribution margin.

Scenario B: ₹18,000 High-Ticket Order Cancellation (Recovering ₹2,746+ in GST)

Now look at a single ₹18,000 order—for instance, an ergonomic office chair, a custom-tailored bridal lehenga, or a bulk corporate gift hamper billed at 18% GST.

  • Taxable Value (Base Price): ₹18,000 ÷ 1.18 = ₹15,254.24
  • Output GST Liability (18%): ₹18,000 − ₹15,254.24 = ₹2,745.76 (rounded to ₹2,746; split as ₹1,372.88 CGST + ₹1,372.88 SGST for intra-state, or ₹2,745.76 IGST for inter-state).
  • Combined Monthly Impact: If you cancel just one ₹18,000 order (₹2,745.76 GST) and one ₹1,499 order at 18% GST (₹228.66 GST), your total recoverable output GST is ₹2,974.42.

Note: If your ₹18,000 quote was exclusive of 18% GST, your invoice total would be ₹18,000 + ₹3,240 = ₹21,240, meaning ₹3,240 in output GST is locked inside that invoice. Either way, you cannot legally claw back that ₹2,746 to ₹3,240 from your GSTR-3B tax payable unless you either cancel the invoice in the same month before filing GSTR-1 (if unshipped) or issue a formal GST Credit Note under Section 34.

Cancel Invoice vs. Credit Note: Which One Should You Use?

Many founders confuse cancelling an invoice with issuing a Credit Note. Here is the clean operational rule based on timing and dispatch status:

Situation Correct Action GSTR-1 Reporting GST Recovered (on ₹18,000 @ 18%)
Order cancelled before dispatch & before GSTR-1 filing for that month Mark invoice CANCELLED in your records; do not reuse the number Omit from B2B/B2C sales tables; report 1 in Table 13 (Cancelled) ₹2,745.76 (never added to output liability)
Order already dispatched, returned (RTO/customer return), or GSTR-1 already filed Issue a sequential GST Credit Note (e.g., CN-2026-019) referencing INV-2026-104 Declare in Table 9B (B2B Credit/Debit Notes) or net off in Table 7 (B2CS) ₹2,745.76 (legally reduces current month output GST)
Wrong GST rate charged (e.g., 18% billed instead of 12% on ₹18,000) Issue a Credit Note for the 6% excess tax difference Report tax reduction in Table 9B / Table 7 Recovers the excess ₹915.25 tax difference
Seller deletes PDF and reuses INV-2026-104 Never do this. Creates audit gap & B2B ITC mismatch Table 13 mismatch triggers ASMT-10 scrutiny Risk of interest @ 18% + penalty

What Every GST Credit Note Must Contain (Section 34 Checklist)

Under Section 34 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 53 of the CGST Rules, a Credit Note is only valid for tax reduction if it includes these mandatory fields:

  1. Document Label: Clearly titled Credit Note at the top (never "Refund Slip" or "Rough Receipt").
  2. Supplier Details: Your legal/trade name, business address, and 15-digit GSTIN.
  3. Unique Sequential Number: A consecutive Credit Note serial number not exceeding 16 characters (for example, CN-2026-019). Keep Credit Note numbering separate from Tax Invoice numbering (INV-2026-105) so neither sequence develops unexplained holes.
  4. Date of Issue: The date you process the cancellation or return acceptance.
  5. Recipient Details: Customer name, address, and GSTIN (mandatory if B2B; optional name/state for B2C).
  6. Original Invoice Reference: The exact serial number and date of the original tax invoice (e.g., Against Invoice No. INV-2026-104 dated 17 Nov 2026). Without this link, the GST portal rejects B2B Credit Notes in Table 9B.
  7. Taxable Value & Tax Breakup: The exact base value being reversed (₹15,254.24) and the split tax amount (₹1,372.88 CGST + ₹1,372.88 SGST, or ₹2,745.76 IGST).
  8. Signature / Digital Sign: Authorized signature or digital stamp of the supplier.

How to Report Credit Notes in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B Without Errors

Once you issue CN-2026-019 for ₹18,000, here is how it flows through your monthly or quarterly GST returns:

1. B2B Registered Buyers (GSTR-1 Table 9B)

If your buyer gave you a 15-digit GSTIN on the original order, upload the Credit Note in Table 9B (Credit/Debit Notes — Registered) by linking it to INV-2026-104. When your buyer opens their GSTR-2B for that month, the portal automatically reduces their eligible Input Tax Credit by ₹2,745.76. Because your output tax reduction matches their ITC reversal to the paisa, both ledgers stay in sync.

2. B2C Unregistered Buyers (GSTR-1 Table 7)

For standard D2C retail orders without a buyer GSTIN (like a ₹1,499 COD return or a ₹18,000 consumer order), you do not upload individual Credit Notes in Table 9B. Instead, you net the Credit Note value off against your state-wise gross B2C sales in Table 7 (B2CS). For example, if you sold ₹1,00,000 taxable B2C goods in Uttar Pradesh at 18% GST and issued ₹15,254.24 in Credit Notes for UP returns, you report net taxable B2C sales of ₹84,745.76 and output GST of ₹15,254.24. Keep the numbered Credit Note PDFs in your monthly folder as proof of the deduction.

3. Watch the Statutory Deadline (November 30th Rule)

Under the amended Section 34(2) of the CGST Act, you cannot issue and declare a GST Credit Note for a financial year's invoice indefinitely. You must declare the Credit Note in your GSTR-1/GSTR-3B no later than 30th November following the end of that financial year (or the date of filing the annual return GSTR-9, whichever is earlier). For any invoice issued between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027, the final cutoff to claim GST reduction via a Credit Note is November 30, 2027. If a customer returns goods after that cutoff, you can still refund them commercially, but you can no longer reduce your output GST liability.
