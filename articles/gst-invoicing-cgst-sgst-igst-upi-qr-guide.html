2. Intra-State (CGST + SGST) vs Inter-State (IGST): The Exact Rule

The single most common billing mistake on B2B and wholesale invoices is charging the wrong tax head. In India, whether you charge CGST + SGST or IGST depends strictly on comparing the Seller's State and the Place of Supply (Buyer's State).

If your workshop or office is registered in Uttar Pradesh (State Code 09) and your buyer is also located in Uttar Pradesh—say, a retail boutique in Lucknow or Kanpur—the transaction is Intra-State. A 12% GST rate must be split equally on the invoice into two lines: 6% Central GST (CGST) and 6% State GST (SGST). On a Rs 10,000 taxable order, your invoice shows Rs 600 CGST and Rs 600 SGST, totaling Rs 11,200.

If your workshop is in Uttar Pradesh (09) and your buyer is in Delhi (07), Maharashtra (27), or Karnataka (29), the transaction is Inter-State. You do not split the tax. You show a single line for Integrated GST (IGST) at the full 12% rate (Rs 1,200). The total payable by the client is identical (Rs 11,200), but getting the tax head right is mandatory so your B2B client can claim Input Tax Credit in their GSTR-2B reconciliation.

  • Same State (e.g., UP to UP): Split 18% GST into 9% CGST + 9% SGST
  • Different State (e.g., UP to Maharashtra): Single line 18% IGST
  • Unregistered / Composition / Exempt Seller: Use 0% Non-GST Bill of Supply mode
  • Check the first two digits of any GSTIN to verify state code (09 = UP, 07 = Delhi, 27 = Maharashtra, 29 = Karnataka)

4. Handling Discounts Without Breaking Tax Calculations

Another place where manual Word or Excel invoices go wrong is trade discounts. Under GST valuation rules, if a discount is agreed upon at or before the time of supply and is clearly shown on the face of the invoice, GST is calculated on the net taxable value after deducting the discount—not on the gross subtotal.

For example, if you bill 10 pairs of handcrafted footwear at Rs 1,200 each (Subtotal = Rs 12,000) and give a flat Rs 1,000 wholesale discount on the invoice, your net taxable value is Rs 11,000. At 12% IGST, the tax is 12% of Rs 11,000 = Rs 1,320, making the final bill Rs 12,320. Calculating tax on Rs 12,000 and then subtracting Rs 1,000 overcharges tax by Rs 120 and creates a mismatch in your buyer's books.

5. HSN and SAC Codes: What Small Sellers & Service Providers Need to Know

Whether you sell physical goods (which use Harmonized System of Nomenclature or HSN codes) or digital and creative services (which use Services Accounting Codes or SAC), putting the right 4-digit or 6-digit classification code on your invoice prevents queries from corporate accounts-payable teams.

For example, footwear generally falls under HSN chapter 64 (such as 6403 for leather footwear or 6404 for textile-upper footwear), corrugated boxes fall under HSN 4819, and software, web design, or marketing consulting falls under SAC 9983. If your annual turnover is up to Rs 5 crore, 4-digit HSN codes are standard for B2B tax invoices; above Rs 5 crore, 6-digit HSN codes are required.

In BillFold, you can include the HSN or SAC code right inside the line-item description—for example, Handcrafted Leather Jooti Pair (HSN 6403) or Website UI & Conversion Setup (SAC 998313). That keeps the invoice layout clean and uncluttered while giving your buyer's chartered accountant every detail needed for GSTR-1 and GSTR-2B matching.

  • Footwear & Leather Goods: HSN 6403 / 6404 (Typically 12% GST up to Rs 1,000 or 12%/18% by slab)
  • Apparel & Readymade Garments: HSN 61 / 62 (Typically 5% up to Rs 1,000 and 12% above Rs 1,000)
  • IT, Web Design & Digital Services: SAC 9983 (Standard 18% GST)
  • Print & Packaging Boxes: HSN 4819 (Typically 12% or 18% GST)

7. Worked B2B vs B2C Invoice Scenarios: Exact Rupee Breakdowns

Let us walk through two concrete billing examples so you never second-guess your invoice totals. Scenario A is a B2B wholesale order: your studio in Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh (State Code 09) sells 25 pairs of embroidered leather jootis at Rs 950 per pair to a retail store in Jaipur, Rajasthan (State Code 08), plus Rs 600 flat insured courier charges. Because the goods move from UP to Rajasthan, this is an Inter-State supply. Under GST rules for composite supply, the freight charged on the invoice takes the same GST rate as the principal goods (12%).

Your subtotal is (25 x Rs 950) + Rs 600 = Rs 23,750 + Rs 600 = Rs 24,350. Suppose you offer a Rs 850 wholesale partner discount on the face of the bill. Your net taxable value is Rs 23,500. You select Inter-State (IGST) at 12% in BillFold, which adds Rs 2,820 in IGST, bringing the grand total to Rs 26,320. Because you included the Jaipur store's 15-digit Rajasthan GSTIN (08...), their accountant can claim the entire Rs 2,820 as Input Tax Credit, meaning their effective cost is only Rs 23,500.

Scenario B is a local B2C studio purchase: a walk-in customer in your own city buys 2 festive pairs at Rs 1,400 each (Rs 2,800 subtotal) with a Rs 200 festive discount (Rs 2,600 taxable base). Because seller and buyer are in the same state, you select Intra-State (CGST + SGST) at 12%. BillFold splits the tax into 6% CGST (Rs 156.00) and 6% SGST (Rs 156.00), printing a clean Rs 2,912.00 receipt with your UPI QR code ready to scan at the counter.
