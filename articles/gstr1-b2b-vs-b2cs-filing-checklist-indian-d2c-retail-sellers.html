GSTR-1 B2B vs B2CS Filing Checklist for Indian D2C & Retail Sellers: Avoiding ₹25,000 Late Fees & Mismatch Notices

November 26, 2026 • 9 min read • KiteDesk Studio Editorial

On the 11th of every month, thousands of Indian D2C brands and retail shop owners log into the GST portal at 10:45 PM, export a messy CSV from their billing sheet, and dump unregistered orders into the wrong GSTR-1 bucket. One month later, a corporate buyer calls because their ₹42,000 Input Tax Credit (ITC) did not show up in GSTR-2B, or an ASMT-10 scrutiny notice lands in your inbox asking why your GSTR-1 turnover does not match GSTR-3B.

The cost of getting GSTR-1 buckets wrong is concrete and measurable in rupees. A 90-day filing delay on a regular return costs ₹4,500 in late fees alone (₹50 per day). Delaying tax remittance on a ₹3,60,000 GST liability by 60 days adds ₹10,652 in Section 50 interest at 18% per annum. Worse, Section 125 of the CGST Act carries a general penalty of up to ₹25,000 for persistent return discrepancies, incorrect invoicing, or failure to furnish accurate HSN summaries. You do not need expensive enterprise ERP software to avoid these penalties—you need a clean pre-filing checklist that separates B2B, B2CL, and B2CS invoices before you hit upload.

1. Why GSTR-1 Classification Breaks for Growing Indian Sellers

When a brand sells ₹1.5 lakh a month on Instagram and WhatsApp, almost every order is a small B2C sale inside the same state or under ₹50,000 across state borders. As soon as you add corporate gifting, wholesale resellers, or high-ticket furniture and electronics orders, your monthly sales register splits into three completely different GST reporting buckets. Mixing them up causes three immediate problems:

  • Blocked Buyer ITC: If a registered business gives you their 15-digit GSTIN and you accidentally push their ₹1,18,000 invoice into B2CS (Table 7) instead of B2B (Table 4A), the buyer cannot claim ₹18,000 of ITC in their GSTR-2B. They will withhold your next payment until you file an amendment in Table 9A next month.
  • False Turnover Mismatches: Reporting a ₹3,00,000 inter-state unregistered order in B2CS instead of B2CL (Table 5A) triggers automated portal flags when e-Way Bill data (mandatory for consignments ≥ ₹50,000) is cross-checked against your return.
  • HSN & Tax Rate Gaps: Rounding off line items or skipping shipping GST in your sales register creates a ₹1,200 to ₹8,500 gap between your GSTR-1 outward tax and your GSTR-3B payment.

2. The Four Core GSTR-1 Tables Explained With Rupee Math

Assume your D2C home-decor brand in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh (State Code 09) closes November 2026 with ₹14,80,000 in gross sales across 412 orders. Here is exactly how those 412 orders must split across the four primary GSTR-1 tables.

Table 4A: Taxable B2B Supplies (Registered Buyers)

Rule: Every supply made to a buyer who provides a valid 15-digit GSTIN goes into Table 4A, regardless of invoice value. Even a ₹450 stationery bill to a GST-registered office belongs in Table 4A, not B2CS.

Worked Example: A boutique hotel in Mumbai, Maharashtra (State Code 27) buys 40 brass table lamps at ₹2,500 each plus ₹2,000 shipping. Your taxable value is ₹1,02,000. Because the supplier is in UP (09) and the place of supply is Maharashtra (27), you charge 18% IGST = ₹18,360. Total invoice value = ₹1,20,360. In Table 4A, you report the exact invoice number, date, buyer GSTIN, place of supply (27-Maharashtra), taxable value (₹1,02,000), and IGST (₹18,360). Because the consignment is ≥ ₹50,000, you also generate an e-Way Bill before dispatch.

Table 5A: B2CL — Inter-State Unregistered Invoices Above ₹2,50,000

Rule: B2CL stands for B2C Large. An invoice belongs in Table 5A only when three conditions are true at the same time: (1) the buyer has no valid GSTIN, (2) the supply is inter-state (IGST applies), and (3) the total invoice value is strictly more than ₹2,50,000.

Worked Example: An individual wedding planner in Jaipur, Rajasthan (State Code 08) orders 100 custom gift hampers at ₹2,500 each without a GSTIN. Taxable value = ₹2,50,000. Adding 18% IGST (₹45,000) brings the total invoice value to ₹2,95,000. Since ₹2,95,000 > ₹2,50,000 and the buyer is in Rajasthan while you are in UP, this single invoice must be reported individually in Table 5A with invoice number, date, place of supply (08-Rajasthan), and taxable value. If that same individual had ordered 80 hampers for ₹2,36,000 total (≤ ₹2,50,000), it would drop into Table 7 (B2CS) instead.

Table 7: B2CS — Consolidated State-Wise Retail & D2C Sales

Rule: Table 7 absorbs everything else that is taxable: all intra-state unregistered sales (regardless of amount—even a ₹4,00,000 intra-state cash/UPI sale to a retail consumer in UP goes to Table 7) plus all inter-state unregistered sales up to and including ₹2,50,000. You do not upload individual invoices here; you upload consolidated net figures grouped by Place of Supply (State) and Tax Rate.

Worked Example: Suppose in November 2026 you shipped 310 prepaid and COD orders to unregistered consumers in Karnataka (29) at 12% GST totaling ₹5,40,000 taxable value (₹64,800 IGST). During the month, 18 COD orders worth ₹32,000 taxable value (₹3,840 IGST) returned to origin (RTO) or were refunded via credit note. In Table 7 for 29-Karnataka (12%), you report the net taxable value: ₹5,40,000 − ₹32,000 = ₹5,08,000, and net IGST = ₹60,960. Keeping RTO returns cleanly separated protects both your tax outflow and your unit economics—check how RTO rates impact net profit per order in MarginMint.

Table 12: HSN-Wise Summary of Outward Supplies

Rule: Every GSTR-1 return must reconcile item-level HSN codes in Table 12 against the sum of Tables 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9. If your aggregate annual turnover in the preceding financial year was up to ₹5 crore, you must report 4-digit HSN codes for B2B supplies (optional for B2C, though recommended for clean audits). If your turnover exceeded ₹5 crore, 6-digit HSN codes are mandatory across all supplies.

Reconciliation Check: Sum the taxable value and tax columns of Table 12. If your Table 4A + Table 5A + Table 7 taxable value equals ₹14,80,000 and total tax equals ₹2,12,400, Table 12 must equal ₹14,80,000 and ₹2,12,400 to the exact rupee. Even a ₹12 rounding mismatch between invoice totals and HSN totals can block portal validation.

3. The Real Rupee Cost of Late Fees, Interest & ₹25,000 Penalties

Many first-time founders assume missing the 11th of the month is harmless if they pay the tax on the 20th in GSTR-3B. Here is the actual cash drain across three statutory sections:

  1. Late Fee (Section 47): Missing the GSTR-1 due date (11th for monthly filers; 13th of the month following the quarter for QRMP) triggers ₹50 per day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST) for regular returns and ₹20 per day (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST) for nil returns. A 30-day delay costs ₹1,500. A 100-day delay on a single return hits ₹5,000 (subject to statutory caps based on turnover, up to ₹2,000–₹5,000 per return depending on turnover slab). Across 6 delayed months, you burn ₹9,000 to ₹30,000 in dead cash.
  2. 18% Interest on Delayed Tax (Section 50): If a GSTR-1 omission delays your GSTR-3B tax payment by 45 days on a ₹4,00,000 net cash liability, interest at 18% p.a. is calculated daily: ₹4,00,000 × 18% × (45 / 365) = ₹8,876.71. Unlike ITC, interest and late fees must be paid strictly in cash through your Electronic Cash Ledger.
  3. General Penalty & Scrutiny Notices (Section 125 & Rule 88C): When your GSTR-1 outward tax exceeds GSTR-3B by more than the portal threshold, Form DRC-01B is issued automatically, giving you 7 days to pay the difference with interest or explain. Unresolved invoicing errors, missing mandatory particulars, or repeated non-compliance can attract a general penalty up to ₹25,000 under Section 125 (CGST) plus equivalent SGST exposure.

4. Pre-Filing 10-Point GSTR-1 Checklist (Run on the 9th of Every Month)

Block 35 minutes on the 9th of every month—two days before the 11th deadline—and run these ten checks in order:

  1. Verify GSTIN Checksum & State Code: Confirm every B2B buyer GSTIN is 15 characters matching the [2-digit state][10-char PAN][1-char entity][Z][checksum] pattern. A single typo moves the invoice into an error state.
  2. Check Intra-State vs Inter-State Tax Split: Confirm your billing address state code matches CGST+SGST for local buyers and IGST for out-of-state buyers. Never charge CGST+SGST on an out-of-state order just because the buyer picked it up at your counter unless Place of Supply is legally your state.
  3. Audit B2CL Threshold (> ₹2,50,000): Filter your sales CSV for all unregistered inter-state invoices above ₹2,50,000 total invoice value and verify they sit in Table 5A, not Table 7.
  4. Reconcile Shipping, Packing & Discounts: Ensure shipping charges collected on the invoice carry the same GST rate as the primary goods (composite supply rule) and trade discounts are deducted before calculating GST.
  5. Net Out Credit Notes & COD RTOs: Match every cancelled or returned order against a numbered Credit Note. For B2B returns, upload the credit note in Table 9B; for B2CS returns, net it off inside Table 7.
  6. Cross-Check E-Way Bills (≥ ₹50,000): Every invoice of ₹50,000 or more involving goods movement must have a matching Part-A and Part-B e-Way Bill number in your dispatch log.
  7. Verify Advance Receipts & Adjustments: For service providers receiving advances before issuing a tax invoice, report unadjusted advances in Table 11A and adjustments in Table 11B.
  8. Validate Document Series (Table 13): Enter your exact starting and ending invoice numbers (for example, INV-2026-101 to INV-2026-512), total issued count, and cancelled count. Cancelled invoices must equal the gap between sequence count and active invoices.
  9. Match Table 12 HSN Totals to ₹0 Variance: Compare Table 12 taxable value and tax totals against Tables 4 + 5 + 7 minus credit notes. Fix any 1-rupee rounding drift using consistent round-off rules in BillFold.
  10. Compare GSTR-1 Preview With GSTR-3B Draft: Before hitting "File Return" with EVC/DSC, download the GSTR-1 summary PDF and verify that total outward tax matches your GSTR-3B Table 3.1(a) figure.

5. How to Collect Missing Buyer GSTINs Before Dispatch

Most B2B-to-B2CS errors happen because a buyer places a prepaid order on WhatsApp or your storefront, forgets to enter their GSTIN, and emails you 12 days later demanding a GST invoice. Solve this upstream before you print the shipping label:

  • Add a clear WhatsApp confirmation prompt on every bulk or corporate inquiry using TapPing so buyers reply with their 15-digit GSTIN and billing name before payment link dispatch.
  • Update your checkout and product FAQ microcopy using CopyCraft to state clearly: "Need a GST Tax Invoice for ITC? Share your 15-digit GSTIN before dispatch; B2C invoices cannot be converted to B2B after the monthly GSTR-1 cut-off."

6. Bottom-Line Monthly Routine

GST compliance does not require a ₹15,000/month retainer for simple D2C and retail workflows if your source invoices are clean. Classify B2B (Table 4A), B2CL > ₹2.5L (Table 5A), and B2CS (Table 7) at the moment of billing, reconcile Table 12 HSN totals on the 9th, file GSTR-1 by the 11th, and keep a 15-day tax cash buffer ready.
