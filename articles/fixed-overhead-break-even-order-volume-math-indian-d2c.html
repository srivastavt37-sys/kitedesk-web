Fixed Overhead & Break-Even Order Volume Math for Indian D2C Brands: Why Unit Profit Still Loses Cash Below 115 Orders

November 23, 2026 • 9 min read • 1760 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

Most Indian D2C founders check unit economics on a single parcel, see ₹305 left over after product cost, GST, shipping, packaging, gateway fees, COD returns, and ad spend, and assume their business is profitable. Then the bank balance drops at the end of the month anyway. That gap between positive per-order profit and a shrinking bank account almost always comes from one missing number: fixed monthly overhead.

Your courier partner charges per parcel, and Meta charges per click, but your studio rent, packing helper salary, software renewals, and CA retainer hit your account on the first of every month whether you ship 40 boxes or 400. If your fixed monthly overhead is ₹35,000 and each blended order leaves ₹305 in unit profit, shipping 100 orders in a month still loses ₹4,500 in real cash. You do not cross into monthly profit until order number 115.

Why Positive Unit Profit Still Bleeds Cash Below 115 Orders

Unit profit (often called CM3 or contribution after ads) only measures what one additional order adds to your cash pool after variable costs. Variable costs move up and down with every parcel: raw material or landed COGS, corrugated box and tape, forward courier weight slab, payment gateway 2% fee, 12% or 18% GST remitted to the government, COD return drag, and customer acquisition cost.

Fixed monthly overhead does not care how many orders you packed this week. Before you keep a single rupee for yourself, your monthly order volume has to pay off every fixed rupee sitting on your desk.

A Realistic ₹35,000 Monthly Fixed Overhead Sheet for a Lean Indian Brand

You do not need a fancy Mumbai office or a 10-person team to carry ₹35,000 in monthly fixed bills. Here is a typical lean setup for a home-grown apparel, skincare, or home-goods brand dispatching out of a small room in Jaipur, Surat, Lucknow, or Bengaluru:

Fixed Monthly Expense What It Covers Monthly Outflow (₹)
Small Studio / Dispatch Room Rent 250 sq ft storage + packing table + shelving ₹12,000
Part-Time Packing & Dispatch Helper 4 hours/day label printing, polybag sealing, courier handover ₹11,500
Storefront, Domain & Connectivity Shopify/store plan, domain renewal reserve, fiber broadband ₹4,500
CA Retainer & GST Filing Monthly GSTR-1, GSTR-3B reconciliation, TDS compliance ₹4,000
Electricity, Thermal Labels & Misc Printer thermal rolls, tape dispenser wear, power bill ₹3,000
Total Fixed Monthly Overhead Paid every month regardless of order count ₹35,000

Notice that none of these five items are wasteful. A part-time packer frees four hours of your afternoon for supplier calls and ad creatives, and a ₹4,000 CA retainer prevents late-fee penalties on GST filings. Yet together they create a ₹35,000 monthly hurdle before the business earns a single rupee of net profit.

The Worked ₹305/Order Unit Profit Breakdown

Now let us build a realistic SKU inside MarginMint so you can see where ₹305 per order comes from. Suppose you sell a handblock cotton kurta set or a ceramic pour-over coffee kit at an MRP of ₹1,199 inclusive of 12% GST, with 40% prepaid UPI orders (offered at a ₹50 prepaid discount) and 60% Cash on Delivery orders carrying an 18% COD RTO rate.

  • Selling Price (GST-Inclusive): ₹1,199.00 on COD orders (₹1,149.00 on prepaid orders after ₹50 UPI discount).
  • Included 12% GST Portion: ₹128.46 on COD (₹123.11 on prepaid). That money belongs to the GST portal, not your margin.
  • Landed COGS: ₹460.00 per piece including stitching, wash, and barcode tag.
  • Forward Courier (500g Volumetric Slab): ₹78.00 average pan-India surface/air blend.
  • Box, Polybag & Thank-You Card: ₹22.00 per parcel.
  • Payment Gateway / COD Handling Fee (2%): ₹23.98 on COD (₹22.98 on prepaid).
  • Blended COD RTO Drag: At 18% RTO on the 60% COD share, roughly 10.8% of total placed orders come back undelivered, burning forward plus reverse shipping (1.8x courier + packaging) across every failed parcel. That spreads an extra ~₹85.00 drag across your blended order pool.
  • Blended Ad CPA: ₹96.50 per placed order across Meta Advantage+ and retargeting.

Once you plug those inputs into MarginMint, your blended unit profit after ad CPA lands right around ₹305.00 per placed order. On paper, ₹305 per order looks healthy—roughly a 25.4% post-ad unit margin on a ₹1,199 ticket.

The Break-Even Formula: Why 100 Orders Loses ₹4,500 and 115 Orders Wins

To find your exact break-even order volume, divide your fixed monthly overhead by your blended unit profit per order, then round up to the next whole parcel:

Break-Even Orders = Math.ceil(Fixed Monthly Overhead / Blended Unit Profit)

Plug in ₹35,000 fixed overhead and ₹305 blended unit profit:

35,000 / 305 = 114.75 orders → 115 orders per month

Look at how sharply the monthly bank balance shifts across five realistic order volumes between 60 and 200 orders a month:

Monthly Orders Unit Profit Pool (Orders × ₹305) Fixed Monthly Overhead True Monthly Net Cash Founder Status
60 orders (2/day) ₹18,300 ₹35,000 -₹16,700 Working capital draining fast
100 orders (3.3/day) ₹30,500 ₹35,000 -₹4,500 Positive unit margin, monthly cash loss
115 orders (3.8/day) ₹35,075 ₹35,000 +₹75 Exact break-even threshold crossed
150 orders (5/day) ₹45,750 ₹35,000 +₹10,750 Fixed rent covered; healthy buffer
200 orders (6.7/day) ₹61,000 ₹35,000 +₹26,000 Every extra order adds pure ₹305 cash

This table explains why so many first-time founders feel confused at 100 orders a month. Every dashboard says they are making ₹305 per box. Every ad manager shows a 4.0x+ ROAS. Yet at 100 orders, they still have to transfer ₹4,500 from personal savings to pay the studio landlord and the CA on the 5th of the next month.

Even more striking is what happens after order 115. Moving from 100 orders to 150 orders is only a 50% bump in parcels—roughly 1.7 extra boxes per day—yet your monthly net profit swings from -₹4,500 to +₹10,750, a ₹15,250 cash improvement. Once fixed overhead is paid off at order 115, 100% of the ₹305 unit profit on order 116 onward drops straight to your bottom line.

Three Ways to Drop Your Break-Even Threshold Without Buying More Ads

When you sit below 115 orders a month, your fastest fix is rarely pouring more money into cold Meta ads (which usually pushes your CPA up from ₹96.50 to ₹145 and shrinks unit profit). Instead, you can lower your break-even order count from both sides of the fraction: lift unit profit per order or trim monthly fixed overhead.

1. Shift COD Orders to Prepaid UPI (+₹58/Order Unit Profit)

Suppose you keep the same ₹1,199 MRP and ₹35,000 fixed overhead, but use a clean WhatsApp confirmation flow via TapPing to nudge 20% of your COD buyers into paying via UPI before dispatch—offering a ₹50 instant UPI discount or free priority dispatch. Dropping your COD share from 60% to 40% cuts your blended RTO burn sharply.

Even after giving away the ₹50 discount on those converted orders, eliminating 1.8x reverse shipping drag on failed deliveries lifts your blended unit profit from ₹305 to roughly ₹363 per order. Recalculate break-even:

35,000 / 363 = 96.4 orders → 97 orders per month

Without spending an extra rupee on ads, your break-even threshold drops by 18 orders per month (from 115 down to 97). At 100 orders a month, instead of losing ₹4,500, you now finish ₹1,300 in the green.

2. Audit Software Subscriptions & SaaS Rent (-₹4,500/Month Overhead)

Many young brands pay ₹1,500/month for a cloud GST invoicing app, ₹1,200/month for a WhatsApp link shortener and QR dashboard, ₹1,000/month for an SEO meta checker, and ₹800/month for a margin tracker. That ₹4,500/month is pure fixed overhead.

Switching routine internal workflows to local-first browser tools eliminates recurring software rent immediately:

  • Issue GST-compliant CGST, SGST, and IGST invoices with instant UPI QR codes and CA-ready CSV registers inside BillFold at ₹0/month.
  • Generate pre-filled wa.me order links and packaging insert QR codes inside TapPing at ₹0/month.
  • Simulate 500g volumetric courier slabs, prepaid vs COD splits, and fixed monthly overhead break-even inside MarginMint at ₹0/month.
  • Check 360px mobile Google SERP titles and strip robotic filler from product descriptions inside CopyCraft at ₹0/month.

Cutting ₹4,500 in fixed SaaS overhead lowers your monthly hurdle from ₹35,000 to ₹30,500. At ₹305 unit profit, your break-even drops immediately from 115 orders to exactly 100 orders per month.

3. Fix Product Copy Clarity to Lift Organic Conversion

If your product pages convert at 1.8% instead of 2.6% because buyers cannot tell fabric GSM, exact dimensions, or dispatch timelines, you end up paying ₹145 CPA instead of ₹96.50 CPA. That ₹48.50 extra ad cost drops your unit profit from ₹305 to ₹256.50—and pushes your break-even volume all the way up to 137 orders per month.

Before launching a new ad set, run your product title and bullet points through CopyCraft to verify that price, material, and delivery window show up cleanly on a 360px Android screen.

The 115-Order Rule Before You Hire or Sign a Lease

Fixed overhead is a ratchet: it is easy to add a ₹12,000 warehouse lease or a ₹15,000 full-time hire, and painful to unwind when sales dip in a quiet month like July or February. Before taking on any new fixed monthly cost, run the extra rupee amount through the break-even formula first.

Every additional ₹10,000 in fixed monthly rent or payroll requires 33 extra profitable orders per month at ₹305 unit profit just to stay flat. Know your exact break-even order count before you scale, protect your cash buffer below 115 orders, and let your unit math dictate when your business is ready for its next fixed commitment.
