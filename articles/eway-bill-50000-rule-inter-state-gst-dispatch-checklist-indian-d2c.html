Then one Tuesday afternoon, a single 4-carton boutique restock or a bulk festive corporate gift order worth ₹44,500 before tax crosses ₹52,510 after 18% IGST and ₹1,200 packing charges. 1. How GST Rule 138 Actually Calculates the ₹50,000 Limit

The biggest mistake small brands make is checking their product subtotal instead of the total invoice value. Under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, the ₹50,000 threshold is measured on the consignment value—meaning the taxable value of goods plus freight, packing, insurance, plus the GST charged on the invoice.

Take a Jaipur apparel studio shipping 40 hand-block cotton kurta sets to a multi-brand store in Bengaluru:

  • Base product value: 40 sets × ₹1,075 = ₹43,000
  • Packing & courier charge billed on invoice: ₹1,200
  • Total taxable value: ₹44,200
  • IGST at 12% (on ₹44,200): ₹5,304
  • Gross invoice total (consignment value): ₹49,504 (No E-Way Bill required)

Now watch what happens if the buyer adds just one extra kurta set (₹1,075 + 12% GST = ₹1,204) or if the SKU falls in the 18% GST slab instead of 12%:

  • 41 sets at 12% GST: ₹45,275 taxable + ₹5,433 IGST = ₹50,708 → E-Way Bill mandatory
  • 40 sets at 18% GST (e.g., accessories or home decor): ₹44,200 taxable + ₹7,956 IGST = ₹52,156 → E-Way Bill mandatory

Founders sometimes gamble on small regional couriers not asking for an E-Way Bill number on ₹55,000–₹75,000 parcels. That gamble breaks down the moment a highway mobile squad scans the truck's consolidated manifest. Under Section 129 of the CGST Act, moving taxable goods worth ₹50,000 or more without a valid E-Way Bill triggers immediate detention of both the goods and the vehicle.

3. Multi-Box Wholesale vs. D2C Parcel Splits: What Is Legal and What Gets Flagged

A common question from D2C operations teams is: "Can we just split one ₹72,000 order into two ₹36,000 invoices so neither needs an E-Way Bill?"

Do not do this for a single buyer shipment moving on the same truck. Under Rule 138(7) and transporter consolidation rules, if multiple invoices belonging to the same consignor and consignee—or even different consignees on the same transporter—exceed ₹50,000 in aggregate value on a single conveyance, GST officers treat intentional invoice splitting on the same day as tax evasion. They will club both ₹36,000 invoices together, see ₹72,000 on the same LR (Lorry Receipt), and issue a Section 129 notice anyway.

Also watch out for inter-state vs. intra-state threshold differences. While the national inter-state threshold is ₹50,000 across all Indian states, a few states enforce different limits or exemptions for intra-state movement (for instance, Delhi has historically exempted certain intra-state movements up to ₹1,00,000, whereas several states stick strictly to ₹50,000). For any inter-state IGST shipment crossing a state border, the ₹50,000 rule is uniform across India—no exceptions for standard taxable goods.

4. Part-A vs. Part-B: Who Fills What Before Courier Pickup

An E-Way Bill has two halves, and 80% of courier delays happen because a founder generates Part-A and forgets Part-B.

  • Part-A (Goods & Invoice Details): Filled by you (the seller) on ewaybillgst.gov.in. It captures your GSTIN, buyer GSTIN (or "URP" for unregistered B2C buyers), invoice number, invoice date, HSN code (minimum 4 digits for turnover up to ₹5 crore, 6 digits above ₹5 crore), taxable value, tax rate, and total invoice value.
  • Part-B (Transport & Vehicle Details): Captures the mode of transport (Road/Rail/Air/Ship), Transporter ID (15-digit GSTIN of Delhivery, Blue Dart, Shiprocket, or your local transport company), and either the Vehicle Number (for direct tempo/truck) or the LR / AWB / Consignment Number + Date.

Critical rule: A Part-A slip alone is not a valid E-Way Bill for highway movement (except for short-distance intra-state movement under 50 km from your warehouse to the transporter's hub). If your courier partner picks up a ₹64,000 box and you only entered Part-A without updating the Transporter ID or AWB in Part-B, the highway squad treats it as missing.

Validity Window Math (200 km Per Day)

Once Part-B is updated, the E-Way Bill clock starts ticking at midnight of the generation day:

  • Up to 200 km: Valid for 1 full day (e.g., generated Nov 25 at 11:00 AM → valid until Nov 26 at 11:59 PM).
  • 201 km to 400 km: Valid for 2 days.
  • 1,200 km (e.g., Delhi to Bengaluru via surface): 1,200 ÷ 200 = 6 days validity.

If a surface shipment gets stuck at a hub due to rain or Diwali backlog and approaches expiry, you or the transporter must extend the validity window on the portal within 8 hours before or after expiry. Letting it expire mid-route triggers the exact same Section 129 penalty as having no E-Way Bill at all.

Print this checklist and tape it above your packing table. Every order over ₹45,000 should pass all five checks before the courier rider scans the barcode:

  2. Verify HSN & state codes: Confirm the buyer's 2-digit GSTIN state code matches your IGST vs. CGST+SGST selection. A mismatch between the PDF invoice and the E-Way Bill Part-A entry is an instant red flag during roadside verification.
