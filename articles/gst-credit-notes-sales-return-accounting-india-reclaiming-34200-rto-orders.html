GST Credit Notes & Sales Return Accounting in India: Reclaiming ₹34,200 in Overpaid Output Tax on Cancelled & RTO Orders

November 27, 2026 • 10 min read • 1943 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

Every month, Indian D2C brands lose lakhs of rupees twice on the same failed order. First, you lose the forward and reverse courier fee when a Cash-on-Delivery (COD) parcel comes back as Return-to-Origin (RTO). Second, you leave 18% GST sitting with the government because your billing sheet recorded the original tax invoice in GSTR-1, but nobody issued a Section 34 GST Credit Note when the parcel returned to your warehouse.

If your store ships 600 orders a month at a 20% RTO rate, that is 120 bounced parcels every 30 days. On a ₹1,593 gross order (₹1,350 taxable value + ₹243 GST at 18%), those 120 failed deliveries carry ₹29,160 in output tax that you never collected from any customer. Add just 10 B2B or bulk corporate cancellations worth ₹2,800 base + ₹504 GST each (₹5,040 tax), and you are overpaying ₹34,200 every single month—or ₹4,10,400 a year—simply because your return accounting stops at the courier dashboard.

1. Why Indian Sellers Overpay Output GST on Cancelled & RTO Parcels

Under Indian GST law, tax liability triggers the moment you generate a Tax Invoice and hand the parcel to your courier partner. Whether you file GSTR-1 monthly or under the QRMP scheme, your sales register exports all issued invoices for that tax period. If you do not generate a formal Credit Note under Section 34 of the CGST Act, 2017, the GST portal assumes every single invoice was delivered, paid for, and consumed.

Many founders assume that marking an order as "Cancelled" or "RTO" inside Shopify, WooCommerce, or Shiprocket automatically adjusts their GST liability. It does not. E-commerce dashboards track order status for logistics, not statutory tax reversal. Unless your accounting register contains a numbered Credit Note linked to the original invoice number and date—and unless that Credit Note is uploaded to GSTR-1 Table 9B—your output tax liability in GSTR-3B stays inflated.

You can inspect this leak in five minutes. Open your last month's sales register in BillFold and compare the total GST collected on dispatched invoices against the actual net delivered revenue in your bank settlement sheet. If your RTO rate sits anywhere between 15% and 28%, the gap between "GST billed" and "GST actually earned" is one of the largest hidden cash drains in your business. Pairing this audit with MarginMint shows how much of your supposed net profit is actually evaporating into unrecovered tax and reverse freight.

3. Section 34 CGST Rules Every Founder Must Follow

Section 34 of the CGST Act gives registered suppliers the legal right to reduce output tax liability when goods are returned or an order is cancelled after invoice issuance. However, the GST department audits Credit Notes closely during scrutiny notices. Follow these five non-negotiable rules so your tax reversal never gets disallowed:

  • Mandatory Reference to Original Invoice: Every Credit Note must state the exact original Tax Invoice number and date. You cannot issue a single lump-sum "Monthly RTO Credit Note" without mapping it to underlying invoices for B2B transactions.
  • Sequential Credit Note Numbering: Maintain a dedicated, continuous series such as CN-2026-001, CN-2026-002, separate from your tax invoice series (INV-2026-104).
  • Reason Code & Return Proof: Record why the Credit Note was issued—such as "Sales Return / RTO Undelivered" or "Post-Dispatch Cancellation"—and keep the courier's RTO POD (Proof of Delivery back to origin) or AWB tracking log in your monthly folder.
  • November 30 Statutory Cutoff: For any invoice issued during a financial year (April to March), the final deadline to issue and report a GST Credit Note in GSTR-1 is November 30 following the end of that financial year (or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier). Miss that cutoff, and you permanently lose the right to adjust output tax.
  • No Duplicate Reduction on Unbilled Drafts: If an order was cancelled before dispatch and you only generated a Proforma Quote (never a Tax Invoice, and never reported it in GSTR-1), do not issue a GST Credit Note. Simply void the proforma quote.

4. GSTR-1 Reporting: Table 9B (B2B) vs. Table 7 Netting (B2CS)

A common mistake among Indian bookkeepers is putting every Credit Note into the same GSTR-1 table. The GST portal splits Credit Notes strictly based on whether the buyer was registered (B2B with GSTIN) or unregistered (B2CS consumer):

Buyer Type Original Invoice Table Credit Note Reporting Rule in GSTR-1 Effect on GSTR-3B
B2B (Registered Buyer with GSTIN) Table 4A / 4B Report individually in Table 9B (CDNR) with Credit Note No., Date, Original Invoice No., and GSTIN. Reduces your output tax; buyer's GSTR-2B automatically reduces their ITC by ₹5,040.
B2CS (D2C Consumer without GSTIN) Table 7 Do not upload individual consumer names in Table 9B. Net the ₹1,62,000 taxable value and ₹29,160 GST directly against state-wise & rate-wise sales in Table 7 (or Table 10 if amending a prior month). Reduces your Table 3.1(a) output tax liability by ₹29,160 in the month of return.
B2CL (Unregistered Inter-State > ₹2.5 Lakh) Table 5 Report individually in Table 9B (CDNUR) against the original B2CL invoice. Reduces inter-state IGST output liability directly.

Notice how clean the B2B vs. B2CS distinction is when you generate documents in BillFold. When you switch BillFold's Document Type dropdown to CREDIT NOTE and enter or clear the Customer GSTIN, the live preview immediately tells you whether the document belongs in CDNR · Table 9B or B2CS · Table 7, alongside the exact Place-of-Supply tax split (CGST + SGST for intra-state vs. IGST for inter-state).

5. Step-by-Step Monthly Workflow: From RTO Manifest to GSTR-3B

Do not wait until the 10th of the next month—one day before the GSTR-1 deadline—to hunt for bounced parcels. Set up a 20-minute weekly close every Saturday morning using these five steps:

  1. Export Weekly RTO Delivered-Back Report: Download the list of all AWBs marked "RTO Delivered" (back at your warehouse) from your shipping aggregator. Never issue a Credit Note while an order is still "In Transit" or "RTO Initiated"—wait until physical stock returns or the courier confirms lost-in-transit settlement.
  2. Match AWB to Original Invoice & Tax Split: Map each AWB to its original Tax Invoice number, invoice date, customer state, and GST rate (5%, 12%, or 18%). If you sold mixed-rate bundles, split the Credit Note line items by HSN rate so your CGST/SGST or IGST totals stay exact.
  3. Issue Sequential Credit Notes in BillFold: Open BillFold, select CREDIT NOTE, use your CN-2026-XXX series, set the matching GST rate and Place of Supply, and mention the original invoice number and RTO AWB in the Invoice Notes field. Save the record to your CSV Sales Register.
  4. Update Inventory & Damaged-Stock Write-Offs: Returned parcels that arrive in saleable condition go back into active SKU stock in StockPulse. If 5% of returned items arrive crushed or unresaleable, mark them as damaged write-offs—and note that under Section 17(5)(h) of the CGST Act, input tax credit (ITC) on goods destroyed or written off must be reversed, whereas tax on the sale that never happened is still validly reversed via the Credit Note.
  5. Reconcile Net Output Tax Before GSTR-3B: Subtract total Credit Note GST (₹34,200) from your gross billed GST before paying challan PMT-06. Feed that recovered ₹34,200 back into your 90-day liquidity plan in CashPulse.

6. Cutting the Problem at the Source: Lowering RTO Before Dispatch

Reclaiming ₹34,200 in overpaid GST protects your cash after an order fails, but preventing 30 to 40 of those 120 RTOs in the first place saves both the courier fee and the accounting overhead. Most Indian D2C brands see an immediate 20% to 30% drop in COD RTOs by tightening two touchpoints before the parcel leaves the table:

  • Automated WhatsApp Order & Pin-Code Confirmation: Within 15 minutes of a COD checkout, send a crisp confirmation message with a prepaid UPI switch incentive (for example, "Pay ₹1,543 via UPI now and save ₹50 instantly"). You can build zero-friction WhatsApp deep links and counter QR standees using TapPing.
  • Clear Delivery-Window & Return-Policy Copy: Ambiguous product descriptions and hidden delivery timelines trigger impulse COD orders that buyers refuse at the doorstep. Audit your product titles, meta descriptions, and WhatsApp catalog blurbs in CopyCraft so buyers know the exact dimensions, dispatch time, and exchange policy before clicking Buy.

Combine both sides of the loop: prevent avoidable COD bounces with TapPing and CopyCraft, price your true RTO-adjusted contribution margin in MarginMint, and issue clean Section 34 Credit Notes in BillFold for every parcel that still comes back. That single monthly discipline keeps ₹34,200 in your operating account where it belongs.
