GST Composition Scheme vs Regular GST Registration in India: Exact ₹20 Lakh to ₹1.5 Crore Margin, ITC, and B2B Client Math

November 13, 2026 • 9 min read • 2032 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

Every Indian small business owner hits the same fork in the road somewhere between ₹20 lakh and ₹40 lakh in annual revenue: should you register under the GST Composition Scheme to pay a flat 1% to 6% tax on turnover, or take Regular GST registration at 12% to 18% and claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on your purchases?

Most accountants give a lazy one-line answer: "Take Composition if you want less paperwork." That single sentence costs growing D2C brands, wholesalers, and agencies lakhs of rupees every year. It also gets them disqualified from B2B corporate contracts before they even send a quote.

This guide breaks down the exact rupee math from ₹20 lakh to ₹1.5 crore in annual turnover, compares 1%, 5%, and 6% Composition rates against 18% Regular GST with ₹1,44,000 in Input Tax Credit on ₹8,00,000 of raw materials and ads, explains the interstate selling ban that traps online sellers, and shows when B2B buyers reject non-ITC bills.

What the GST Composition Scheme Actually Is (and Who Can Use It)

The GST Composition Scheme under Section 10 of the CGST Act is a simplified tax option for small businesses whose aggregate annual turnover in the preceding financial year did not exceed ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for special category states like Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and most North-Eastern states). For pure service providers, the turnover cap is ₹50 lakh.

Under Composition, you do not charge GST on your customer's invoice. Instead, you issue a "Bill of Supply" and pay a small flat percentage out of your own pocket (or bake it into your MRP) every quarter:

  • Manufacturers and Traders (Goods): 1% of turnover (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST). Note that traders pay 1% only on taxable supplies, whereas manufacturers pay 1% on total turnover.
  • Restaurants (not serving alcohol): 5% of turnover (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST).
  • Service Providers (up to ₹50 lakh turnover): 6% of turnover (3% CGST + 3% SGST).

In exchange for that low rate and quarterly CMP-08 filing, you give up three major rights: you cannot claim Input Tax Credit on anything you buy, you cannot charge GST separately on your bills, and you cannot sell goods across state borders or through e-commerce operators like Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho that collect TCS.

The Core Math: 1% to 6% Composition Turnover Tax vs 18% Regular GST with ITC

To see which scheme leaves more cash in your bank account, you cannot look at the output tax rate alone. You have to look at your gross margin and how much GST you already pay to your suppliers, packaging vendors, courier partners, and ad platforms.

Let us run a concrete worked example for an Indian business doing ₹24,00,000 in annual revenue (₹2,00,000 per month) with ₹8,00,000 in GST-billed inputs (raw materials, packaging boxes, Meta/Google ads, and software tools) where suppliers charge 18% GST.

Case A: Goods Manufacturer / D2C Brand (1% Composition vs 18% Regular GST)

Suppose you sell leather goods or home hardware locally within Uttar Pradesh for ₹24,00,000 a year. Your raw materials, 3-ply boxes, and Meta ad spend total ₹8,00,000 base cost, plus 18% GST = ₹1,44,000 in GST paid to vendors (total vendor outflow ₹9,44,000).

Under 1% Composition Scheme:

  • Revenue collected from buyers: ₹24,00,000 (you issue a Bill of Supply with zero GST line item).
  • Input cost paid to vendors: ₹8,00,000 + ₹1,44,000 GST = ₹9,44,000. You get ₹0 ITC back, so the ₹1,44,000 GST becomes a permanent cost.
  • Composition tax paid to government (1% of ₹24,00,000): ₹24,000.
  • Total tax burden eaten by your business: ₹1,44,000 (unclaimed vendor GST) + ₹24,000 (turnover tax) = ₹1,68,000.
  • Net cash left before rent and salaries: ₹24,00,000 - ₹9,44,000 - ₹24,000 = ₹14,32,000.

Now compare 18% Regular GST on the exact same ₹24,00,000 base sales (assuming B2B or price-elastic buyers pay ₹24,00,000 + 18% GST = ₹28,32,000 total):

  • Output GST collected from buyers (18%): ₹4,32,000.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC) claimed on ₹8,00,000 inputs: ₹1,44,000.
  • Net GST paid in cash to government: ₹4,32,000 - ₹1,44,000 = ₹2,88,000.
  • Net cash left before rent and salaries: ₹24,00,000 - ₹8,00,000 (ex-GST input cost) = ₹16,00,000.

Look at that gap: ₹16,00,000 vs ₹14,32,000. That is ₹1,68,000 more profit in Regular GST when your customer pays the GST on top (standard in B2B). Even if you sell B2C at a fixed MRP of ₹24,00,000 inclusive of tax, let us check the inclusive math: base value = ₹24,00,000 / 1.18 = ₹20,33,898, output GST = ₹3,66,102, minus ₹1,44,000 ITC = ₹2,22,102 net GST paid, leaving ₹20,33,898 - ₹8,00,000 = ₹12,33,898. In B2C price-sensitive retail where buyers will not pay 18% extra, 1% Composition wins by ₹1,98,102. That single distinction (can you pass GST to the buyer, or do you absorb it in MRP?) decides the entire math.

Case B: Agency / Freelancer / Consultant (6% Composition vs 18% Regular GST)

Now look at a digital marketing agency, web studio, or consulting practice doing ₹24,00,000 a year, spending ₹8,00,000 on ads, SaaS subscriptions, and freelance contractors (with ₹1,44,000 ITC available):

  • Under 6% Composition: You pay 6% of ₹24,00,000 = ₹1,44,000 out of your pocket, plus you lose ₹1,44,000 in ITC. Total tax drag = ₹2,88,000. Net cash left = ₹24,00,000 - ₹9,44,000 - ₹1,44,000 = ₹13,12,000.
  • Under 18% Regular GST (B2B clients): Your clients claim the 18% GST as their own ITC, so they gladly pay ₹24,00,000 + ₹4,32,000 GST. You offset ₹1,44,000 ITC and keep ₹16,00,000 net cash.

For a B2B service provider with ₹8,00,000 in GST-billed inputs, the 6% Composition Scheme costs you ₹2,88,000 every single year in lost margin compared to Regular GST. You can verify your exact unit economics after GST and payment gateway fees using MarginMint.

When B2B Buyers Reject Non-ITC Bills (The Procurement Reality)

Even if you are a small trader selling locally, the moment you pitch a corporate office, a hotel chain, a distributor, or a funded D2C brand, your Bill of Supply becomes a deal-breaker.

Here is why a B2B buyer's finance team rejects a Composition invoice. Suppose a Regular GST vendor quotes ₹1,00,000 + 18% GST = ₹1,18,000. The buyer pays ₹1,18,000, immediately claims ₹18,000 as Input Tax Credit on their GSTR-2B, and their true net cost is ₹1,00,000.

Now suppose you, as a Composition dealer, quote ₹1,05,000 (zero GST shown on the Bill of Supply). On paper, ₹1,05,000 looks ₹13,000 cheaper than ₹1,18,000. In reality, the buyer gets ₹0 ITC, so their true cost is ₹1,05,000, which is ₹5,000 more expensive than the Regular GST vendor. To match the Regular GST vendor's effective price, you have to cut your quote below ₹1,00,000 while still eating your own unclaimed input GST and 1% turnover tax.

On top of price, enterprise procurement portals literally block onboarding if you cannot upload a valid Regular GSTIN and file GSTR-1 so your invoice reflects in their GSTR-2B every month. When you switch to Regular GST, generating clean B2B invoices with proper CGST/SGST or IGST splits and a scannable UPI QR code takes under 30 seconds in BillFold.

The Interstate Selling Trap for D2C and WhatsApp Sellers

The biggest operational shock for small Indian brands on Composition is Section 10(2)(c) of the CGST Act: a Composition dealer cannot make any interstate outward supply of goods.

Think about what that means in 2026:

  • If your shop is in Jaipur (Rajasthan) and a customer in Pune (Maharashtra) orders a ₹1,800 pair of jootis on WhatsApp or Instagram, you are legally barred from shipping it under the Composition Scheme.
  • You can buy raw materials from another state (interstate inward supply is allowed), but you cannot ship a single finished parcel outside your state.
  • If you sell on Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho, marketplaces deduct 0.5% TCS and require Regular GST registration for interstate fulfillment (though intra-state e-commerce supply rules have relaxed slightly for micro-sellers, you still lose national shipping reach).

If you run Instagram Reels or local SEO pages that bring in orders via TapPing WhatsApp links from customers across Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, and Hyderabad, you need Regular GST registration so you can issue valid IGST invoices legally across state lines.

Compliance Cost: Quarterly CMP-08 vs Monthly GSTR-1 & GSTR-3B

The one genuine advantage of Composition is lower compliance cost and time:

  • Composition Scheme: 4 quarterly CMP-08 tax payments + 1 annual GSTR-4 return (5 filings per year). Typical CA retainer in Tier-2 Indian cities: ₹4,000 to ₹6,000 per year.
  • Regular GST (QRMP Scheme, under ₹5 crore): Monthly GSTR-1 / IFF (for B2B invoices) and quarterly GSTR-3B tax payment. Typical CA retainer: ₹12,000 to ₹18,000 per year.

The difference in CA fees is roughly ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 per year. Compare ₹12,000 in extra CA fees against ₹1,44,000 in recovered Input Tax Credit or ₹3,00,000+ in lost B2B contracts. Saving ₹1,000 a month on accounting while throwing away ₹12,000 a month in ITC is false economy.

Decision Checklist: Which Scheme Fits Your Business in 2026?

Choose the GST Composition Scheme only if ALL four of these conditions are true:

  1. 100% of your sales happen inside a single state (zero interstate shipping).
  2. Your customers are end consumers (B2C) who do not ask for GST input credit, such as a local bakery, kirana store, salon, or neighborhood restaurant.
  3. Most of your suppliers are unregistered or exempt (for example, fresh farm produce or unbranded local labor), so you have very little 12% or 18% ITC to lose.
  4. Your annual turnover is comfortably below ₹1.5 crore (goods) or ₹50 lakh (services).

Choose Regular GST Registration immediately if ANY of these apply:

  1. You ship D2C orders across state borders via Shiprocket, Delhivery, or India Post.
  2. You sell to B2B buyers, corporates, wholesalers, or agencies that demand a tax invoice for GSTR-2B credit.
  3. You spend heavily on Meta/Google ads (18% GST), packaging, SaaS tools, or GST-registered raw material vendors where ITC exceeds ₹30,000 a year.
  4. You plan to list on marketplaces or scale past ₹1.5 crore.

How to Execute the Switch Without Breaking Your Cash Flow

If you are currently under Composition and losing B2B deals or interstate orders, you can opt out voluntarily by filing Form GST CMP-04 on the GST portal. You also get to claim ITC on your closing stock held on the day before you switch by filing Form GST ITC-01 within 30 days (verified by a CA if the claim exceeds ₹2 lakh).
