E-Way Bill ₹50,000 Threshold & E-Invoicing Rules in India: Avoiding ₹10,000 Transit Penalties on B2B & D2C Shipments

A Surat textile wholesaler packs 40 cotton kurtis for a boutique in Jaipur. The taxable value on the invoice is ₹46,428.57, plus 12% IGST of ₹5,571.43. The grand total is ₹52,000. Because the base price sat below ₹50,000, the dispatch clerk skips the E-Way Bill portal and hands the carton to a regional road carrier. Twelve hours later, a GST flying squad stops the truck near the Rajasthan border. 1. The ₹52,000 Interstate Consignment Math: Why Taxable Value Doesn't Save You

Under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, 2017, an E-Way Bill (Form GST EWB-01) is mandatory whenever the consignment value of goods in transit exceeds ₹50,000. The law defines consignment value as the transaction value declared on the tax invoice, delivery challan, or bill of supply, including CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST, and Cess, and excluding only the value of exempt supplies if both taxable and exempt goods share the same invoice.

Look at how the math works on an interstate B2B shipment from Surat (Gujarat) to Jaipur (Rajasthan):

Taxable Value (Before GST) ₹46,428.57 ₹44,067.80
IGST Rate & Tax Amount 12% = ₹5,571.43 18% = ₹7,932.20
Total Consignment Value (Invoice Total) ₹52,000.00 ₹52,000.00

Section 122(1)(xiv) Minimum Penalty ₹10,000.00 ₹10,000.00

Notice the margin wipeout. If your net profit on that ₹52,000 wholesale lot was 14% (₹6,500 on the ₹46,428.57 base), a single Section 129 detention penalty of ₹11,142.86 erases the entire profit from that order plus 71% of the profit from your next order. The Split-Invoice Myth That Triggers Detention

Some dispatch teams try to stay under the ₹50,000 limit by splitting one ₹52,000 order into two ₹26,000 invoices placed inside the same tempo or courier manifest. High Courts and GST appellate authorities routinely uphold detention on split invoices when both invoices move in the same conveyance for the same buyer on the same day. Tax officers aggregate the consignment value when the underlying movement is a single commercial transaction. 2. Part-A vs. Part-B Rules and Transporter GSTIN Responsibilities

Form GST EWB-01 has two halves, and confusion between them causes more highway delays than missing paperwork altogether.

  • Part A (Consignment Details): Filled by the supplier (or recipient if buying from an unregistered dealer). It captures your GSTIN, the buyer's GSTIN, delivery PIN code, invoice or challan number and date, total consignment value, 4-digit or 6-digit HSN code, reason for transportation (Supply, Export, Job Work, Sales Return), and the transport document number (LR/GR/AWB number).
  • Part B (Vehicle & Transporter Details): Captures the mode of transport (Road, Rail, Air, Ship), vehicle registration number, and the Transporter ID / GSTIN. An E-Way Bill is not valid for movement with only Part A filled—unless the distance from your warehouse to the transporter's godown (or from the transporter's hub to the buyer) is up to 50 km within the same state.

Who Updates Part B When You Use a 3PL or Road Carrier?

There are three real-world handoff patterns in Indian B2B and D2C logistics:

  1. Own Vehicle or Dedicated Tempo: You fill both Part A and Part B before the vehicle leaves your gate. Enter the exact truck number (for example, GJ05BX4821) without spaces.
  2. Registered Road Transporter (VRL, TCI, Safexpress, Regional LTL): You fill Part A and enter the carrier's 15-digit Transporter GSTIN (or 15-character TRANSIN if the transporter is unregistered under GST) in the Transporter ID field. That authorizes the transporter to log into the E-Way Bill portal and update Part B with the line-haul truck number at their hub.
  3. D2C Aggregators (Shiprocket, Delhivery, Blue Dart): Most API-integrated aggregators pull your invoice value and generate or update Part B automatically once you enter the 12-digit E-Way Bill Number (EBN) or enable auto-EWB in your panel. Never assume auto-generation worked—verify the 12-digit EBN and its validity window before handing over the manifest.

If your dispatch team coordinates pickup slots and LR numbers with buyers over WhatsApp, standardize the handoff message using TapPing. Send a pre-filled WhatsApp confirmation containing the invoice number, 12-digit EBN, and tracking link so buyers never flood your support line asking where their truck is.

3. Validity Clock: The 200-Kilometre Rule and Mid-Transit Expiry

Every E-Way Bill carries a strict expiry clock measured from the exact timestamp of Part-B generation, where each day equals 24 hours:

  • Regular Cargo: Valid for 1 day for every 200 km (or part thereof). A 180 km run gets 1 day; a 450 km run gets 3 days.
  • Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) or Multimodal Shipment (where at least one leg is by ship): Valid for 1 day for every 20 km (or part thereof).

Suppose your Surat-to-Jaipur shipment covers 910 km. Five blocks of 200 km (up to 1,000 km) give you 5 days of validity. If a highway blockade or truck breakdown delays the vehicle past midnight on day 5, the transporter must extend the validity on the portal between 8 hours before expiry and 8 hours after expiry. Driving even four hours past an expired E-Way Bill exposes the consignment to Section 129 detention. Keep a simple dispatch log and check active EBNs every morning at 9:00 AM.

4. Section 129 vs. Section 125 Penalties: Knowing the Exact Financial Exposure

Since January 1, 2022 (via Finance Act, 2021 amendments), Section 129 of the CGST Act shifted from collecting "tax plus 100% penalty" to a pure penalty structure designed to deter missing documentation:

  • Owner Comes Forward (Section 129(1)(a)): Penalty is 200% of the tax payable on taxable goods. For exempted goods, it is 2% of the value of goods or ₹25,000, whichever is less.
  • Owner Does Not Come Forward (Section 129(1)(b)): Penalty jumps to the higher of 50% of the value of goods or 200% of the tax payable. On our ₹52,000 lot (₹46,428.57 base + ₹5,571.43 tax), if the owner stays silent, 50% of value is ₹26,000—more than double the ₹11,142.86 penalty when the owner comes forward immediately in writing.
  • Minor Clerical Typo (CBIC Circular 64/38/2018-GST): If the invoice and E-Way Bill are otherwise valid, but there is an error in 1 or 2 digits of the vehicle number or a minor PIN code typo (where distance does not change materially), officers should levy ₹500 CGST + ₹500 SGST (₹1,000 total) under Section 125 rather than seizing the truck under Section 129.

Also watch the statutory clock if a truck is stopped: the detaining officer must issue a notice (FORM GST MOV-07) within 7 days of detention and pass the final order within 7 days of serving the notice. Never leave a transporter to negotiate alone at a check post; step in immediately with your GSTIN, invoice PDF from BillFold, and written owner representation.

5. The ₹5 Crore E-Invoicing (IRN) Threshold: Why It Matters Even on ₹12,000 Orders

Many growing D2C brands treat E-Way Bills and E-Invoicing as separate silos. In practice, they lock together once your business crosses ₹5 Crore in Aggregate Annual Turnover (AATO).

Effective August 1, 2023, and continuing in FY 2026–27, e-invoicing is mandatory for every GST-registered business whose AATO exceeded ₹5 Crore in any preceding financial year from 2017–18 onwards. Notice three strict traps in that rule:

  1. PAN-Level Aggregation: Turnover is totalled across all GSTINs linked to the same PAN across India, including taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports, and interstate stock transfers. Two warehouses doing ₹2.8 Crore each in Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh equal ₹5.6 Crore—triggering mandatory e-invoicing for both states.
  2. One-Way Ratchet: If your turnover hit ₹5.2 Crore in FY 2023–24 and dipped to ₹4.1 Crore in FY 2025–26, you are still legally required to generate e-invoices because the ₹5 Crore threshold was crossed in a prior year.
  3. Applies to All B2B & Export Invoices (No ₹50,000 Floor): Unlike the E-Way Bill, e-invoicing has no minimum invoice value. Even a ₹1,200 B2B invoice or credit note requires an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and signed QR code from the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). B2C retail invoices to end consumers without a GSTIN do not need an IRN, though they still need an E-Way Bill if a single B2C shipment exceeds ₹50,000.

What Happens If a ₹5 Crore+ Supplier Ships Without an IRN?

Under Rule 48(5) of the CGST Rules, an invoice issued by a mandated taxpayer without a valid IRN and QR code is not a valid tax invoice. Your B2B buyer cannot claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on GSTR-2B, and your invoice will fail automated e-Way Bill auto-population on the IRP. Corporate procurement teams now reject non-IRN PDFs automatically, freezing your receivables for weeks.

Even if your turnover is currently ₹1.5 Crore—well below the ₹5 Crore mandate—clean invoicing habits protect your cash flow today. 6. Pre-Dispatch Compliance Checklist for B2B & D2C Teams

Tape this six-point check above your packing table before dispatching any B2B wholesale lot or high-value D2C order:

  • Check Total Invoice Value (Including GST): If Taxable Value + GST exceeds ₹50,000, generate Form GST EWB-01 before the vehicle moves. Never rely on base taxable value alone.
  • Verify Recipient GSTIN & PIN Code: Confirm the buyer's 15-character GSTIN and delivery PIN code match the tax invoice. A mismatch in state code switches CGST+SGST to IGST and invalidates the E-Way Bill.
  • Confirm Part B Before Gate-Out: Either enter your truck number directly or enter the carrier's 15-character Transporter ID and verify Part B is updated before the line-haul truck leaves the city hub (only intra-state movement under 50 km to the transporter is exempt from Part B).
  • Audit 4-Digit / 6-Digit HSN Codes: Ensure HSN codes on the invoice match the E-Way Bill portal entry.
  • Check ₹5 Crore AATO Status: If total PAN-level turnover exceeded ₹5 Crore in any year since 2017–18, generate the IRN first on the IRP and auto-generate the E-Way Bill from the IRN JSON.
  • Send Clean Dispatch & Payment Links: Attach the PDF invoice from BillFold, share the tracking and EBN update via TapPing, and make sure your B2B wholesale landing page copy in CopyCraft states GST billing and dispatch timelines clearly so buyers know exactly what paperwork they will receive.

They happen because someone misread ₹46,428.57 as "under ₹50,000," forgot to verify Part B with a regional transporter, or let an E-Way Bill expire during a 900 km highway run. Build the ₹50,000 check and the 15-day MSME payment term into your daily invoice workflow once, and you eliminate ₹10,000+ roadside surprises permanently.
