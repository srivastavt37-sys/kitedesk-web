B2B Wholesale Invoicing & Proforma Quotes in India: Turning ₹25,000+ Bulk Inquiries into Paid Orders Without Credit Risk

November 17, 2026 • 11 min read • 2256 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

A WhatsApp message lands at 4:15 PM: "Need 100 units for our Pune store opening next week. Send your best wholesale rate and GST bill, we will clear payment in 30 days." Most small Indian manufacturers, D2C wholesalers, and boutique suppliers get excited by the ₹25,000 to ₹85,000 order size, say yes to 30-day credit on the spot, and spend the next two months chasing their own money on WhatsApp.

Open 30-day credit is the fastest way for a profitable small supplier to run out of cash. Even when a buyer is honest, "30 days" in Indian B2B trade routinely stretches to 48 or 60 days while you still have to pay your raw-material vendors, staff salaries, and monthly GST liability in cash. You do not need to turn away ₹25,000+ bulk inquiries to protect yourself. You just need a clean proforma quote system and a 40/60 dispatch-linked payment rule.

This playbook gives you the exact rupee math for ₹25,000 and ₹85,000 wholesale orders, shows why 40% advance proforma plus 60% against Lorry Receipt (LR) or courier tracking ID beats 30-day open credit, explains how the ₹50,000 e-Way Bill threshold affects your dispatch workflow, and lays out the exact WhatsApp dispatch proof sequence using BillFold, TapPing, MarginMint, and CopyCraft.

1. Why 30-Day Open Credit Quietly Wrecks Small Supplier Cash Flow

When you ship goods on 30-day open credit, you are acting as an unpaid bank for your buyer. Three hidden costs hit your bank account before you see a single rupee back:

  • GST cash outflow before collection: The moment you raise a final Tax Invoice in a given month, your output GST becomes payable in GSTR-3B by the 20th of the following month. If you ship an ₹85,000 + 18% GST order on November 17 on 30-day credit, you must deposit ₹15,300 in GST to the government by December 20—even if the buyer delays your payment until January.
  • Working capital lockup: Your fabric, leather, packaging boxes, and labour are paid upfront in cash or 7-day terms. Financing 100% of the buyer's inventory for 30 to 60 days prevents you from taking two other cash-paying orders in the meantime.
  • Zero leverage after delivery: Once the transporter hands over the cartons at the buyer's godown, your leverage drops to zero. Follow-up calls turn into "accountant is on leave" or "stock is moving slowly."

Before quoting any wholesale discount, run your unit cost, GST slab, and packaging overhead through MarginMint so you know your exact net rupee margin at wholesale volume rather than guessing from retail MRP.

2. The Rupee Math: ₹25,000 vs ₹85,000 Bulk Orders (30-Day Credit vs 40/60 Rule)

Compare how two real Indian wholesale orders play out under 30-day open credit versus the 40% Advance on Proforma + 60% Against Lorry Receipt (LR) / Tracking ID rule.

Scenario A: ₹25,000 Bulk Order (50 Pairs of Footwear / Apparel at ₹500 + 12% GST)

Suppose a regional boutique orders 50 units at ₹500 each (HSN 6403 or HSN 6205, 12% GST). Taxable value is ₹25,000, GST (12% IGST or 6% CGST + 6% SGST) is ₹3,000, and the total invoice value is ₹28,000. Assume your direct production and box cost is ₹16,500 (₹330 per unit) and your net profit before tax is ₹8,500.

  • Under 30-day open credit: You spend ₹16,500 in cash to manufacture and pack the 50 units, plus ₹3,000 in GST liability next month. Total cash out of pocket before collecting a rupee: ₹19,500. If the buyer defaults or stalls for 60 days, you lose ₹19,500 in hard cash plus ₹8,500 in profit.
  • Under 40% Proforma + 60% Against LR: You issue a Proforma Quote for ₹28,000 and collect 40% (₹11,200) via UPI or NEFT before cutting a single piece. That ₹11,200 funds 68% of your ₹16,500 production cost immediately. When the cartons are packed and booked with the transporter, you send the photo of the sealed boxes plus the LR/tracking slip on WhatsApp and collect the remaining 60% (₹16,800) before issuing the final delivery release.

Scenario B: ₹85,000 Bulk Order (100 Skincare / Corporate Gift Kits at ₹850 + 18% GST)

Now scale up to a ₹85,000 order—say, 100 skincare or corporate gifting sets at ₹850 each (HSN 3304, 18% GST). Taxable value is ₹85,000, 18% GST is ₹15,300, and total invoice value is ₹1,00,300. Assume your direct manufacturing and rigid-box packaging cost is ₹54,000.

  • Under 30-day open credit: You put ₹54,000 of production cash and ₹15,300 of GST liability on the line—a ₹69,300 cash exposure on a single buyer. At a 15% annual working-capital borrowing rate, even a normal 45-day collection cycle costs you roughly ₹1,280 in interest, eating straight into your margin.
  • Under 40% Proforma + 60% Against LR: A 40% advance brings in ₹40,120 on day one. Your net cash out of pocket during production drops from ₹69,300 to just ₹13,880. On dispatch day, collecting the remaining 60% (₹60,180) against the Lorry Receipt closes your credit exposure to ₹0 before the truck even crosses the state border.

3. Proforma Quote vs Final Tax Invoice: Never Mix Them Up

Many small businesses make a costly accounting mistake: they generate a final Tax Invoice to ask for an advance, send the PDF on WhatsApp, and then the buyer ghosts them. Now that invoice number sits in your sequence, and if you already reported it or forgot to cancel it properly, your GST reconciliation gets messy.

Keep the two documents strictly separate:

  1. Proforma Quote (PQ) for the 40% Advance: A Proforma is a formal quotation and payment request, not a GST supply invoice. Mark the document header or notes clearly as "PROFORMA QUOTE — 40% Advance Required to Confirm Production", include the exact HSN/SAC codes, unit rates, GST breakdown, and your UPI QR / bank details. You can build and export this in under 30 seconds inside BillFold using the HSN/SAC Quick-Fill selector and custom Payment Terms.
  2. Final Tax Invoice for Dispatch & 60% Balance: Only convert the Proforma into a numbered Tax Invoice (e.g., INV-2026-042) once production is finished and the cartons are ready to load. Save every invoice in BillFold's local history and back it up with the Export JSON button so your team never loses a quote or invoice sequence when switching browsers.

If your buyer asks why you need a formal Proforma first, the answer is simple: their own accounts team needs an itemized quote with your GSTIN, HSN code, and total rupee amount in words to approve the NEFT/IMPS transfer.

4. The ₹50,000 e-Way Bill Threshold Rules You Must Check Before Dispatch

Notice the sharp difference between our ₹25,000 order and our ₹85,000 order in the table above. Under Indian GST rules, an e-Way Bill is mandatory whenever the total consignment value (taxable value plus GST) exceeds ₹50,000 for inter-state movement of goods (and for intra-state movement above the state's notified threshold, which is ₹50,000 in most states, though some states set ₹1,00,000 or ₹2,00,000 for intra-state).

  • Why ₹28,000 stays simple: For the ₹25,000 + 12% GST = ₹28,000 order, the total consignment value is below ₹50,000. You can ship immediately via Delhivery, Blue Dart, India Post, or local transport with just your printed BillFold Tax Invoice inside the pouch—no e-Way Bill portal login required under the central ₹50,000 rule.
  • Why ₹1,00,300 requires an e-Way Bill: For the ₹85,000 + 18% GST = ₹1,00,300 order, shipping without an e-Way Bill exposes your goods to detention and a penalty equal to 100% of the tax or ₹10,000 (whichever is higher) under Section 129 of the CGST Act.

To generate the e-Way Bill cleanly on the NIC portal without holding up the tempo at your gate:

  1. Keep HSN codes accurate on the invoice: Your BillFold invoice already tags the 4-to-8 digit HSN code (such as 6403 for Footwear or 3304 for Skincare) and splits CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state).
  2. Split Part A and Part B cleanly: Enter the buyer's GSTIN, invoice number, taxable value, and HSN in Part A. Ask your transporter or courier for their Transporter ID (if shipping via surface LTL like VRL, TCI, or Safexpress) or vehicle number for Part B before the truck leaves city limits.
  3. Watch the "Bill To – Ship To" trap: If a buyer's registered GST office is in Mumbai (Maharashtra) but they want the 100 gift boxes delivered to their Pune warehouse or an event venue, fill the billing GSTIN under "Bill To" and the delivery address under "Ship To" on the e-Way Bill so the tax officer sees a valid route.

5. The WhatsApp Dispatch Proof Workflow That Collects the 60% Balance Fast

Buyers rarely delay the 60% balance because they want to cheat you; they delay because they are not sure their goods actually left your warehouse. Visual proof on WhatsApp removes all doubt and triggers instant NEFT or UPI settlement.

Follow this 4-step dispatch proof ritual for every wholesale parcel:

  1. Photo 1 — Open Carton Verification: Snap one clear daylight photo of the packed units inside the master carton before taping, showing count/layers and protective bubble wrap.
  2. Photo 2 — Sealed Carton with Shipping Mark: Tape the carton with branded or cross-strapped tape, write the buyer's city, order number, and "Box 1 of 4" clearly with a black marker, and take a photo.
  3. Photo 3 — Lorry Receipt (LR) / Courier AWB Slip: As soon as the transporter hands you the LR copy or courier receipt with the tracking number and weight, click a crisp photo showing the destination city and LR number.
  4. Send the 1-Tap Payment Pack: Attach the final Tax Invoice PDF from BillFold (which already includes your dynamic UPI QR code and exact Indian Rupee amount in words), plus a pre-filled WhatsApp payment link or QR card from TapPing for fast mobile settlement.

Exact WhatsApp Dispatch Proof Script (Copy & Use)

"Hi [Buyer Name] ji, your wholesale order ([Order/Invoice No.]) is packed and handed over to [Transporter/Courier Name] today. Attached above: (1) Sealed carton photos (4 master boxes, total 38.5 kg), (2) Lorry Receipt / AWB #[LR Number] to [Destination City], and (3) Final GST Tax Invoice for ₹[Total] (including e-Way Bill #[EWB Number]). Advance received: ₹[40% Amount]. Balance payable against LR: ₹[60% Amount]. You can scan the UPI QR on the invoice or transfer via NEFT to [Account Details]. Please share the UTR screenshot once done so we can confirm final delivery release with the transport office!"

Notice the final line: "confirm final delivery release with the transport office." With LTL transporters (like VRL, Gati, or regional tempo services), you keep the original Consignee Copy of the LR until the 60% UTR hits your bank account—or instruct the transporter to hold delivery against "To-Pay / Consignee Release." That single operational habit gives you 100% security without sounding rude.

6. How to Handle "We Only Do 30 Days Credit" Objections

When a new B2B buyer pushes back and insists on 30-day open credit on their first order, do not argue about trust. Give them a structured, respectful way to earn credit over time:

  • Script for First-Time Wholesale Buyers: "We keep our wholesale margins razor-thin so you get the best retail markup—that is why all first 3 orders run on 40% advance proforma and 60% against Lorry Receipt. Once we complete 3 smooth cycles together, we can review a 15-day credit limit for repeat restocks."
  • Offer a 2% Cash-on-Dispatch Discount: If a buyer genuinely wants better terms, raise your base wholesale quote by 2% and offer a "2% Early Settlement Discount" when the 60% balance is cleared within 4 hours of sharing the LR photo. Buyers love feeling like they won a 2% discount, and you get paid on dispatch day.
  • Polish Your Wholesale Pitch Line: Keep your catalog intro and MOQ/payment terms crisp—under 150 words with zero fluff—using CopyCraft so new buyers understand your pricing slabs, GST billing, and 40/60 dispatch terms before they even ask for credit.

7. Quick Wholesale Readiness Checklist Before Your Next ₹25,000+ Quote

Run through these five checks before replying to your next bulk inquiry on WhatsApp:

  • Verify your true net profit per unit at wholesale rate (including 3-ply/5-ply master carton cost and forward freight) in MarginMint.
  • Generate a clean Proforma Quote in BillFold using the HSN/SAC Quick-Fill selector (12% for HSN 6403/6205, 18% for HSN 3304/4819 or SAC 998314/998361) and set Payment Terms to 40% Advance / 60% Against LR.
  • Check whether total invoice value (goods + GST) crosses ₹50,000 so you factor in e-Way Bill generation before dispatch.
  • Export your BillFold saved invoices JSON backup once a week so your quote and invoice history stays safe across browser updates.
  • Send your 3-photo WhatsApp dispatch proof + LR number immediately upon booking so the 60% balance clears the same afternoon.

Wholesale growth does not come from chasing bigger top-line numbers on paper; it comes from collecting cash on time without burning your own working capital. Lock in the 40/60 proforma-to-LR workflow once, and every ₹25,000 or ₹85,000 bulk inquiry becomes predictable cash flow instead of a 60-day collection headache.
