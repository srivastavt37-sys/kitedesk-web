TDS Under Section 194J & 194C for Indian Freelancers & Agencies: Exact ₹30,000 Threshold, 10% vs 2% Cut, and Net Bank Credit Math

November 19, 2026 • 9 min read • 1763 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

You finish a branding sprint for a Bengaluru SaaS startup, raise a clean ₹1,00,000 tax invoice plus 18% GST (₹1,18,000 total) using BillFold, and wait for the NEFT alert. Three days later, your bank account receives ₹1,06,200 instead of ₹1,08,000. The client's accounts clerk deducted 10% TDS on the entire ₹1,18,000 gross bill—including the ₹18,000 GST component—costing you ₹1,800 in immediate working capital.

That ₹1,800 leak happens every week across Indian design studios, performance marketing agencies, software contractors, and solo consultants. Worse, most freelancers do not catch it until July when they open their Annual Information Statement (AIS) or Form 26AS, long after the client has filed their quarterly TDS return.

You do not need a chartered accountant on speed dial to stop TDS leaks. You need three exact numbers memorized: the ₹30,000 threshold rule, the difference between Section 194J (10% or 2%) and Section 194C (1% or 2%), and the rule that separates base fees from GST before any percentage is multiplied.

1. The Core ₹1,00,000 + 18% GST Invoice Math (Preventing the ₹1,800 Over-Deduction)

Under CBDT Circular No. 23/2017, if GST on services is shown separately on your tax invoice, TDS must be deducted strictly on the base service value—never on the GST amount. When you issue an invoice in BillFold, the line-item subtotal and the CGST/SGST or IGST tax lines are already separated cleanly so your client's finance team has zero excuse to calculate TDS on the gross total.

Here is the exact side-by-side math on a ₹1,00,000 professional fee with 18% GST under Section 194J (10% TDS):

Invoice & Settlement Line Correct Math (TDS on Base Only) Common Client Mistake (TDS on Gross)
Base Service Fee (Taxable Value) ₹1,00,000 ₹1,00,000
GST @ 18% (CGST 9% + SGST 9% or IGST 18%) ₹18,000 ₹18,000
Total Tax Invoice Value ₹1,18,000 ₹1,18,000
TDS @ 10% (Section 194J) ₹10,000 (10% of ₹1,00,000) ₹11,800 (10% of ₹1,18,000)
Net Bank Credit Received by You ₹1,08,000 ₹1,06,200
Working Capital Lost / Locked Until Refund ₹0 ₹1,800 per ₹1L invoice

If your agency bills ₹6,00,000 a month across six ₹1,00,000 retainers, that 1.8% error locks up ₹10,800 every single month—or ₹1,29,600 over a financial year. You still owe ₹18,000 to the GST portal by the 20th of the next month regardless of what the client deducted. When a client cuts ₹11,800 instead of ₹10,000, you are funding that ₹1,800 gap out of your own pocket until your Income Tax Return (ITR) refund arrives months later.

What If Your Invoice Has Out-of-Pocket Reimbursements?

Suppose you run Meta Ads or print packaging boxes for a client and add ₹40,000 in actual third-party reimbursements onto a ₹1,00,000 retainer bill. If you lump the ₹40,000 into your agency fee line, the client will cut 10% TDS on ₹1,40,000 (₹14,000 instead of ₹10,000). Always separate pure reimbursements with supporting vendor bills—or better yet, have the client pay the ad account or printer directly—so your TDS applies only to your actual service margin.

2. Section 194J vs Section 194C: 10% vs 2% (and 1%) Cut

Clients often default every agency or freelancer to 10% TDS under Section 194J because it is the safest conservative option for their own tax audit. That single classification choice changes your monthly cash flow by 8% to 9%.

Section Who & What It Covers TDS Rate (With Valid PAN) Rate Without PAN (Sec 206AA) Annual Threshold
Section 194J(b) Professional services: legal, CA, architecture, interior design, medical, consulting, brand strategy, copywriting, freelance UI/UX 10% 20% ₹30,000 per financial year (per service category)
Section 194J(a) Technical services (not being professional services) and royalty for sale/distribution of cinematographic films or call-centre operations 2% 20% ₹30,000 per financial year
Section 194C (Individual / HUF) Contract work: printing, event production, video editing/shoot crew, catering, logistics, white-label manufacturing 1% 20% ₹30,000 single bill OR ₹1,00,000 aggregate in a FY
Section 194C (Company / LLP / Firm) Same contract work billed by a Private Limited, LLP, or Partnership agency 2% 20% ₹30,000 single bill OR ₹1,00,000 aggregate in a FY

Why Agencies Get Over-Deducted on Mixed Work

Imagine you run a small D2C creative studio structured as an LLP. You sign a ₹2,00,000 contract to shoot product photos, edit 12 reels, and print 5,000 thank-you inserts. Printing and physical production fall under Section 194C (2% TDS = ₹4,000), whereas pure consulting falls under Section 194J (10% TDS = ₹20,000). That is a ₹16,000 cash-flow swing on a single project.

Before you send the final tax invoice, clarify the scope wording on your BillFold Proforma Quote and final invoice. If your work is physical production, printing, or contract execution, state the deliverable as contract production rather than "advisory consultancy." And if you are pricing retainers to protect take-home cash, run your gross margin and TDS buffer through MarginMint before quoting.

3. The Exact ₹30,000 Threshold Rule (And Why March Bills Trigger Retroactive Cuts)

Many freelancers assume that if an invoice is ₹25,000, no TDS applies. That is only half true. The ₹30,000 threshold works differently under Section 194J and Section 194C:

  • Section 194J (₹30,000 cumulative per financial year): There is no separate single-bill limit. The ₹30,000 cap applies to the total amount credited or paid to you during the financial year (April 1 to March 31) for each category (professional fees, technical fees, or royalty). If you bill ₹20,000 in June and ₹15,000 in October to the same client, the total hits ₹35,000. The client must deduct 10% TDS on the full ₹35,000 (₹3,500) when paying the October ₹15,000 bill—leaving you with ₹11,500 net plus GST.
  • Section 194C (₹30,000 single OR ₹1,00,000 annual aggregate): TDS triggers if a single contract payment exceeds ₹30,000, OR if total contract payments to you in the financial year cross ₹1,00,000. Four separate ₹22,000 printing bills (₹88,000 total) stay below ₹1,00,000 and trigger ₹0 TDS. The moment a fifth ₹22,000 bill pushes the annual total to ₹1,10,000, TDS applies on the entire ₹1,10,000.
  • Individual / HUF Client Exemption: An individual or HUF client whose turnover did not exceed ₹1 crore (business) or ₹50 lakh (profession) in the preceding financial year is not required to deduct TDS under 194C or 194J for personal or small-scale payments.

Always check your client's ledger total with you in February and March. Knowing when you cross ₹30,000 (194J) or ₹1,00,000 (194C) prevents surprise 20% cash drops on your final quarter invoice.

4. How to Spot and Fix TDS Errors in AIS and Form 26AS

Even when a client deducts the right ₹10,000 on a ₹1,00,000 invoice, mistakes happen when their accountant files Form 26Q. If they mistype one character of your PAN or deposit the money under the wrong section, the ₹10,000 credit will not appear on your tax profile—and the Income Tax Department will ask you to pay tax on that ₹10,000 a second time.

Your 15-Day Post-Quarter Reconciliation Checklist

TDS returns are filed quarterly: Q1 (Apr–Jun) by July 31, Q2 (Jul–Sep) by October 31, Q3 (Oct–Dec) by January 31, and Q4 (Jan–Mar) by May 31. Within 15 days after each due date, run these four checks on the Income Tax e-filing portal:

  1. Open Form 26AS and AIS side by side: Log in to the Income Tax portal and check both Form 26AS (Part A) and the AIS TDS/TCS Information tab. Confirm every client's TAN, total amount paid/credited (which must match your base taxable value, NOT your GST-inclusive total), and TDS deposited.
  2. Verify the Section Code (194J vs 194C): If you are a contract videographer billed under 194C (1% or 2%) but the client reported it under 194J, flag it so your ITR income nature matches your actual business activity.
  3. Check for Unmatched Deductions: If a client cut ₹10,000 from your bank payout in August, it must show up in Q2 Form 26AS by mid-November. If it is missing, they either failed to deposit the challan or entered the wrong PAN in Form 26Q.
  4. Request Form 16A (PDF): Every deductor is legally required to issue Form 16A downloaded from TRACES within 15 days of filing their quarterly return. Save each Form 16A in a dedicated FY folder alongside your BillFold invoice PDFs.

What to Do When a Client Deducted ₹11,800 Instead of ₹10,000

If you catch the ₹1,800 GST over-deduction within the same quarter before the client files Form 26Q, send a polite two-line note with your next invoice or WhatsApp follow-up. You can generate a clean, clickable WhatsApp payment reminder link in TapPing and attach a crisp summary written with CopyCraft:

"Hi [Name], thanks for settling INV-2026-104. Quick note: ₹10,000 TDS (10% on ₹1,00,000 base) applies here rather than ₹11,800, as GST (₹18,000) is excluded from TDS per CBDT Circular 23/2017. Could you release the ₹1,800 balance or adjust it in our next bill?"

If the client has already deposited ₹11,800 against your PAN and filed Form 26Q, do not waste weeks arguing for a reversal. Verify that the full ₹11,800 reflects in your Form 26AS. You will get full credit for all ₹11,800 against your annual income tax liability—or receive it back as an ITR refund—as long as it is sitting cleanly against your PAN.

5. Five Invoice Habits That Stop TDS Disputes Before They Start

  • Never bundle GST into a single lump-sum figure: Always show Base Taxable Value, CGST/SGST or IGST, and Grand Total on separate lines. BillFold enforces this layout automatically on every A4 PDF.
  • Print your PAN directly below your GSTIN: A missing PAN triggers mandatory 20% TDS under Section 206AA. Keep your 10-character PAN visible in your payment notes on every bill.
  • Use Proforma Quotes before raising a Tax Invoice: When a corporate client needs internal PO approval, send a Proforma Quote first using BillFold's Document Type selector so you do not owe GST on an unpaid or cancelled draft.
  • Track net bank credit, not gross invoice value: When forecasting monthly cash flow in MarginMint, model your real bank inflow (Base − TDS + GST) and keep the GST portion in a separate tax sub-account so you never spend it by mistake.
  • Send structured WhatsApp reminders on due dates: Pair your invoice PDF with a pre-filled TapPing WhatsApp link and a clear payment-terms note from CopyCraft so accounts teams process your payout in the first weekly payment run rather than pushing it to next month.

Getting TDS right is not about memorizing tax law—it is about making your invoice layout impossible to misread. Separate your base fee from GST, confirm whether your work is 194J (10%/2%) or 194C (1%/2%), reconcile Form 26AS every quarter, and keep every ₹1,800 in your operating account where it belongs.
