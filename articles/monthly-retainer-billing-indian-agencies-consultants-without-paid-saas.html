Why Retainer Invoices Fail Audit Checks (And How Sequential Numbering Fixes It)

Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, every tax invoice issued by a GST-registered Indian business must carry a consecutive serial number unique for a financial year, not exceeding 16 characters. When agencies duplicate Word or Google Docs files manually, two mistakes happen constantly:

  • Duplicate invoice numbers: You duplicate INV-2026-104.docx for Client B, update the client name and amount, and forget to change the header from 104 to 105. Your CA catches it three months later during GSTR-1 reconciliation, forcing credit notes and amended filings.
  • Stale invoice dates: You send a November 1 invoice that still reads "October 1, 2026" in the date field. The client's accounts payable team rejects it on the 10th, delaying your ₹18,000 payout by two more weeks.

Inside BillFold, the Duplicate as Next # button in the Saved Invoices bar solves both errors in a single click. Load any client's previous month bill from your browser's LocalStorage chips, click Duplicate as Next #, and the tool immediately increments the trailing digits of your invoice number (for example, turning INV-2026-104 into INV-2026-105), resets the invoice date to today's local date, recalculates the 15-day due date, and regenerates both the GST tax split and the live UPI QR code.

Handling GST, IGST, and Section 194J TDS on ₹18,000 Retainers

Indian B2B retainers come with two tax wrinkles that generic global invoicing tools get wrong: intra-state vs. inter-state GST splits, and Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 194J or 194C.

Intra-State (CGST + SGST) vs. Inter-State (IGST)

If your studio is registered in Maharashtra (state code 27) and your client is in Pune or Mumbai (also 27), you must split 18% GST into 9% CGST + 9% SGST (₹1,620 + ₹1,620 = ₹3,240 on an ₹18,000 retainer, totaling ₹21,240). If your client is in Bengaluru (state code 29) or Delhi (07), you must charge 18% IGST (₹3,240). Switching the Tax Mode dropdown in BillFold updates the invoice preview and tax summary table instantly without rebuilding the line items.

Accounting for 10% TDS Under Section 194J

Most corporate and funded startup clients deduct 10% TDS under Section 194J on professional and technical retainers (2% under 194C for certain production contracts). Remember a critical rule: TDS is calculated on the base retainer value (₹18,000), never on the GST portion.

Retainer Component Amount (INR) Tax / Payout Note
Monthly Retainer Fee (Base) ₹18,000.00 Taxable service value
GST at 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST or 18% IGST) ₹3,240.00 Remitted to GST portal by you
Gross Invoice Total ₹21,240.00 Booked in your GSTR-1 sales register

Across 15 clients at ₹18,000 each, your gross billed revenue is ₹3,18,600/month, your net cash received after 10% TDS is ₹2,91,600/month, and ₹27,000 sits as TDS credit you claim or refund at year-end. Set the TDS Rate field in BillFold so your invoice footnote shows the exact net payable amount—stopping accounts teams from accidentally deducting 10% on the ₹21,240 gross figure (which would short-pay you ₹324 per client, or ₹4,860/month across 15 clients).

UPI QR Codes vs. NEFT/RTGS: Getting Paid Faster on Retainers

For retainers between ₹10,000 and ₹50,000, adding a dynamic UPI QR code directly on the PDF invoice cuts payment follow-ups in half. Founders and small-business clients routinely pay ₹18,000 to ₹21,240 bills straight from GPay, PhonePe, or Paytm Business in 15 seconds rather than logging into corporate net-banking to add a new NEFT beneficiary.

Keep two practical limits in mind:

  • Standard UPI transaction cap: Regular P2M/P2P UPI transfers cap at ₹1,00,000 per day on most Indian banks. For retainers up to ₹80,000 + GST, UPI QR works smoothly. For ₹1,50,000+ enterprise retainers, keep your NEFT/RTGS bank details in the invoice Notes field.
  • TDS-adjusted QR amount: If a B2B client deducts 10% TDS before paying, mention both the Gross Total (₹21,240) and Net Payable after TDS (₹19,440) clearly on the invoice so there is zero confusion when they scan.
