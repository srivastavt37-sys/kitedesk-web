Indian GST State Codes (01–38) & Place of Supply Rules: Preventing CGST/SGST vs IGST Filing Errors

November 22, 2026 • 9 min read • 1847 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

One wrong two-digit prefix on a tax invoice can freeze your working capital for months. Every Indian GSTIN starts with a two-digit state code between 01 and 38. That single pair of digits tells the GST portal whether an order is intrastate (within the same state, split equally into CGST + SGST) or interstate (crossing a state border, billed as IGST). When a seller or accountant guesses the state code or leaves an invoice template stuck on CGST + SGST for an out-of-state buyer, the portal does not let you quietly swap the tax heads later.

Under Section 77 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Section 19 of the IGST Act, 2017, if you pay CGST and SGST on a transaction that was legally an interstate supply, you still owe the full IGST amount to the government. You must deposit the IGST separately and file a refund claim (Form GST RFD-01) for the wrongly paid CGST and SGST. Until that refund clears, you have paid double tax on the exact same invoice.

1. The ₹35,000 B2B Invoice Mistake: How One Wrong State Code Locks ₹6,300

Let us walk through a real B2B service or wholesale scenario. Suppose your agency or studio is registered in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh (State Code 09) and you bill a client in Mumbai, Maharashtra (State Code 27) for a ₹35,000 monthly retainer or wholesale batch. The applicable GST slab is 18%.

  • Correct Interstate Billing (09 → 27): Taxable value = ₹35,000. Since 09 (Uttar Pradesh) and 27 (Maharashtra) are different states, you must charge 18% IGST = ₹6,300. Total invoice value = ₹41,300.
  • The Common Template Error: Your billing sheet was left on the default intrastate setting from your last local Lucknow client. You issue the invoice showing 9% CGST (₹3,150) + 9% SGST (₹3,150) = ₹6,300. Total invoice value is still ₹41,300, so neither you nor the client notices the mistake on PDF review.

Here is what happens at month-end when your client's CA reconciles GSTR-2B and your CA files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B:

  1. Blocked Input Tax Credit (ITC) for Your Client: A Maharashtra buyer (27) cannot claim SGST paid to the Uttar Pradesh government (09). Their CA rejects the invoice and holds your ₹41,300 payout—or demands a credit note and fresh IGST invoice.
  2. No Cross-Head Offset in GSTR-3B: If you already filed GSTR-3B and paid ₹3,150 CGST + ₹3,150 SGST, you cannot transfer that ₹6,300 balance into the IGST ledger on the GST portal.
  3. Section 77 Double-Tax Cash Lockup: To fix GSTR-1/GSTR-3B, you must pay ₹6,300 in IGST again using your electronic cash or credit ledger, and then apply for a ₹6,300 refund of the wrongly paid CGST + SGST via Form RFD-01. That locks ₹6,300 of your own cash for 60 to 120 days while the refund application moves through officer scrutiny.

On a single ₹35,000 invoice, ₹6,300 is 18% of your top-line revenue sitting idle. If you run a 25% net margin on that retainer (₹8,750 profit), a ₹6,300 tax lockup wipes out 72% of your profit cash flow for that month until the refund hits your bank account.

2. The ₹1,499 D2C Order Math: Why Small Retail Slips Compound Fast

B2C and D2C sellers often assume Place of Supply errors only matter for large B2B invoices. They hurt even faster at high order volumes. Consider a D2C brand shipping from Jaipur, Rajasthan (08) to a customer in Bengaluru, Karnataka (29) at a tax-inclusive selling price of ₹1,499.

First, separate the taxable base from the GST component accurately:

  • At 12% GST (e.g., apparel or footwear above ₹1,000): Taxable value = ₹1,499 ÷ 1.12 = ₹1,338.39. GST = ₹160.61.
  • At 18% GST (e.g., leather accessories, electronics, personal care): Taxable value = ₹1,499 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,270.34. GST = ₹228.66.

Now suppose a misconfigured Shopify plugin, WhatsApp billing sheet, or manual CSV upload logs 100 interstate ₹1,499 orders (at 18% GST) as intrastate Rajasthan sales (9% CGST + 9% SGST instead of 18% IGST). Each order carries ₹228.66 of misclassified tax. Across 100 orders, that is ₹22,866 deposited into the wrong tax heads.

Before you absorb that hit, check your unit economics in MarginMint: on a ₹1,499 D2C order with ₹480 landed COGS, ₹95 forward shipping, and ₹140 customer acquisition cost, your net contribution profit is roughly ₹555 per order. A ₹22,866 Section 77 cash lockup ties up the net profit of 41 delivered orders in one filing cycle. Worse, if you also run COD and face a 20% RTO rate, you are paying ₹160 in two-way shipping on failed parcels while waiting on tax refunds.

4. Five Place of Supply Traps That Break CGST/SGST vs IGST Logic

Matching the first two digits of Seller GSTIN and Buyer GSTIN solves 85% of invoices. The remaining 15% fail because Place of Supply under Sections 10 and 12 of the IGST Act depends on where goods move or where a service is consumed, not just the billing address on a letterhead.

Trap 1: Bill-To / Ship-To Across Different States

Suppose your supplier in Delhi (07) sells goods to your company in Noida, Uttar Pradesh (09), but asks the courier to deliver the shipment directly to your customer's warehouse in Gurugram, Haryana (06). Under Section 10(1)(b) of the IGST Act, the Place of Supply between the supplier and you is your principal place of business (09 – Uttar Pradesh), so the supplier bills IGST (07 → 09). Your second leg of billing to the Gurugram customer is also IGST (09 → 06). Never let a vendor charge Delhi CGST+SGST just because the pickup happened in Delhi.

Trap 2: B2C Customers Without a GSTIN

When an individual consumer buys a ₹1,499 item on your website or via TapPing WhatsApp checkout, they have no GSTIN. Place of Supply is the delivery state on the shipping label. If your warehouse is in Gujarat (24) and the parcel ships to Pune, Maharashtra (27), you must charge 18% IGST even though the customer is unregistered. If the customer walks into your Ahmedabad store and carries the bag out in person, delivery ends at your counter (24 → 24), so you charge 9% CGST + 9% SGST.

Trap 3: Multi-State Warehouses (FBA, Shiprocket, or Delhivery Fulfillment)

If you store inventory in a third-party fulfillment center in Karnataka (29) while your registered office is in Uttar Pradesh (09), you must hold an Additional Place of Business or separate GSTIN in Karnataka (29). When that Karnataka warehouse ships an order to a buyer in Bengaluru (29), the supply is intrastate (29 → 29), so it attracts CGST + SGST—not IGST from your UP headquarters.

Trap 4: Hotel, Event, and Immovable Property Services

If a Mumbai (27) founder travels to Jaipur (08) for a trade expo and stays at a hotel, the Place of Supply for accommodation and event-venue services under Section 12(3) is the physical location of the property (08 – Rajasthan). The hotel correctly charges Rajasthan CGST + SGST. You cannot claim ITC on that Rajasthan SGST in Maharashtra (27), which is normal—do not ask the hotel to re-bill it as IGST.

Trap 5: Typo in State Code Passing Basic Length Checks

A 15-character string like 99ABCDE1234F1Z5 or 28XYZAB5678G1Z2 has the right length and PAN structure, so weak regex validators mark it green. Yet 99 is not an Indian state code, and 28 is the deprecated pre-division Andhra Pradesh code. Checking the two-digit prefix against the live 01–38 table catches dead or mistyped codes before the PDF leaves your desk.
