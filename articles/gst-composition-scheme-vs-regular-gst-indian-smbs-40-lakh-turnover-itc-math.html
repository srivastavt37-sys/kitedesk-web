GST Composition Scheme vs Regular GST for Indian SMBs: Exact ₹40 Lakh Turnover Tax & ITC Math

November 26, 2026 • 8 min read • 1893 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

Most Indian shop owners and first-time D2C founders hear two numbers when they cross ₹20 lakh or ₹40 lakh in annual sales: 1% under the GST Composition Scheme versus 12% or 18% under Regular GST. On paper, paying 1% looks like an easy win. Who would pay 18% when 1% is legal?

The catch is hidden inside your purchase bills, courier invoices, and customer list. Under Composition, that 1% comes straight out of your pocket, you get zero Input Tax Credit (ITC) on the GST you pay to suppliers, you cannot issue a tax invoice to B2B buyers, and you cannot ship goods across state borders or sell on most national marketplaces. At ₹40 lakh annual turnover, those restrictions regularly cost a growing seller ₹1.40 lakh to ₹2.88 lakh per year in lost credits and missed orders.

Let us run the exact rupee math for a ₹40,00,000 annual turnover business so you can pick the right GST structure before filing your next return.

1. The Two Rules That Actually Decide Your Choice

Before touching a calculator, check the two legal gates under Section 10 of the CGST Act that separate Composition from Regular GST:

  • Turnover Cap: Goods traders and manufacturers can opt for Composition up to ₹1.5 crore annual aggregate turnover (₹75 lakh in specified special-category states). Service providers under Section 10(2A) are capped at ₹50 lakh and pay 6% (3% CGST + 3% SGST), not 1%.
  • Inter-State & Marketplace Ban: A Composition dealer cannot make inter-state outward supplies of goods. If you sit in Jaipur and ship a ₹1,299 kurti to a buyer in Pune, or list your SKU on Amazon, Flipkart, or Myntra for pan-India fulfillment, Composition is off the table. You must register as a Regular taxpayer regardless of whether your turnover is ₹8 lakh or ₹80 lakh.

If 100% of your sales happen inside one state to walk-in retail consumers—like a neighbourhood kirana store, local bakery, hardware counter, or single-city salon—Composition stays on the table. The moment you ship to another state, sell to GST-registered businesses, or buy high-GST inventory, you need to compare the actual cash flow.

2. The ₹40 Lakh Turnover Benchmark: Side-by-Side Rupee Math

Assume an Indian SMB does ₹40,00,000 in annual sales (roughly ₹3.33 lakh per month). To keep the comparison honest, let us look at two realistic cost structures: a Product Reseller / D2C Brand buying taxable stock, packaging, and courier services, and a Local High-Margin Retailer buying mostly exempt or low-tax local inputs.

Scenario A: D2C Brand or Trader with ₹24 Lakh Taxable Inputs (12% Avg GST)

Suppose your annual revenue target is ₹40,00,000 (excluding GST on Regular invoices, or total bill value under Composition). Your yearly supplier bills—raw materials, corrugated boxes, labels, software, and courier freight—total ₹24,00,000 before tax. At an average 12% GST rate (or ₹16,00,000 of 18% GST inputs like packaging, machinery, and ads), your vendors charge you ₹2,88,000 in GST across the year.

Line Item (Annual ₹40 Lakh Turnover) Composition Scheme (1%) Regular GST (12% Output) Regular GST (18% Output)
Customer Invoice Value ₹40,00,000 (Bill of Supply) ₹40,00,000 + ₹4,80,000 GST ₹40,00,000 + ₹7,20,000 GST
Gross Output GST Liability ₹40,000 (1% of ₹40L) ₹4,80,000 ₹7,20,000
Input Tax Credit (ITC) Claimed ₹0 (Blocked) ₹2,88,000 Recovered ₹2,88,000 Recovered
Net GST Paid in Cash ₹40,000 ₹1,92,000 ₹4,32,000

Total Tax + Unrecovered GST Cost ₹3,28,000 ₹1,92,000 (Collected from Buyer) ₹4,32,000 (Collected from Buyer)

Read that bottom row twice. Under Composition, your CA files a 1% return for ₹40,000, so it feels cheap. In reality, you already paid ₹2,88,000 to your suppliers and courier partners in GST that you cannot claim back. Your true tax cost embedded in your P&L is ₹3,28,000—and you are not allowed to collect a single rupee of GST separately from your customer.

Under Regular GST at 12%, you recover the full ₹2,88,000 ITC. If your market lets you charge ₹40,00,000 + 12% GST (which is standard for B2B orders and branded D2C SKUs), your net profit jumps by ₹2,88,000 per year compared to Composition because your input cost drops from ₹26,88,000 (₹24L + ₹2.88L) back down to ₹24,00,000.

What If You Cannot Raise Your Retail Price? (MRP-Locked B2C Math)

Now test the tougher case. Suppose you sell an MRP-locked consumer item at ₹1,000 tax-inclusive, and buyers refuse to pay ₹1,120. On 4,000 units sold in a year, total cash collected from customers is ₹40,00,000 either way.

  • Under Composition (1%): Gross receipts = ₹40,00,000. Composition tax = ₹40,000. Supplier cost including 12% GST = ₹26,88,000. Net gross margin before rent and salaries = ₹40,00,000 - ₹40,000 - ₹26,88,000 = ₹12,72,000.
  • Under Regular GST (12% Inclusive): Taxable base value = ₹40,00,000 / 1.12 = ₹35,71,429. Output GST = ₹4,28,571. Less ₹2,88,000 ITC = ₹1,40,571 net cash GST paid. Supplier base cost = ₹24,00,000. Net gross margin = ₹35,71,429 - ₹24,00,000 = ₹11,71,429.

Here is the exact breakeven insight most founders miss: when prices are 100% MRP-locked and B2C buyers do not claim ITC, Composition saves you ₹1,00,571 per year at 12% output GST (and even more if your output slab is 18%, where inclusive tax is ₹6,10,169). You can model your exact SKU-level inclusive vs exclusive GST pricing inside MarginMint in under 30 seconds before locking your retail MRP.

3. Where Composition Breaks Completely: B2B Buyers & Marketplaces

The MRP-locked B2C advantage vanishes the moment you deal with business customers or pan-India shipping. Look at three real situations where staying on Composition kills deals:

A. The B2B Corporate or Wholesale Order

Imagine a local corporate office in Bengaluru wants to buy 500 gift hampers from you at ₹1,000 each (₹5,00,000 order). Under Regular GST, you bill ₹5,00,000 + 18% GST = ₹5,90,000. The corporate buyer claims ₹90,000 as ITC, so their real cost is ₹5,00,000. Under Composition, you issue a Bill of Supply for ₹5,00,000 with zero ITC. Even worse, if your own packaging and dry-fruit inputs cost ₹3,20,000 + ₹48,000 GST, you absorb that ₹48,000 GST plus ₹5,000 Composition tax. Meanwhile, a Regular GST competitor quotes ₹4,90,000 + GST, pockets ITC, and wins the tender.

B. Inter-State D2C Orders & E-Way Bill Compliance

An Instagram Reel goes viral and 45% of your ₹40 lakh revenue comes from buyers outside your home state. A Composition dealer is legally barred from making inter-state outward supplies of goods. Trying to ship from Delhi to Mumbai on a Composition GSTIN invites penalties and stock detention.

Once you are on Regular GST, inter-state orders require IGST instead of CGST + SGST, and any single consignment exceeding ₹50,000 needs an E-Way Bill under GST Rule 138. C. Marketplace Selling (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho)

To sell GST-taxable goods across India on major marketplaces, you need a Regular GSTIN (the limited intra-state e-commerce Composition relaxation still blocks pan-India shipping and requires marketplace-specific compliance). Plus, marketplaces deduct 0.5% TCS (Tax Collected at Source) and charge 15%–22% referral and closing fees plus 18% GST on their commission. If you pay ₹3,60,000 in annual marketplace and ad fees, 18% GST on those fees equals ₹64,800—which is 100% recoverable as ITC only under Regular GST.

4. Compliance Cost & Cash Flow Impact: Quarterly vs Monthly

Many small founders pick Composition because they fear monthly CA fees. Let us put real numbers on compliance and working capital:

  • Composition Compliance: You pay tax quarterly via CMP-08 (due 18th of the month following the quarter) and file one annual return (GSTR-4). Typical CA retainer: ₹6,000 to ₹10,000 per year.
  • Regular GST (QRMP Scheme): If your turnover is under ₹5 crore, you do not have to file 36 returns a year. Under the Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme, you file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly while paying estimated tax monthly via PMT-06. Typical CA retainer: ₹15,000 to ₹24,000 per year.

That extra ₹10,000 to ₹14,000 per year in CA fees is negligible if Regular GST recovers ₹1,50,000 to ₹2,88,000 in ITC or opens up ₹10 lakh in inter-state and B2B revenue. However, watch your cash flow timing: under Regular GST, if you sell on 30-day B2B credit or 10-day COD remittance cycles, you may owe output GST on the 20th of next month before the customer's money hits your bank. 5. Decision Checklist: Which Scheme Fits Your ₹40 Lakh Business?

Use this five-point filter before you register or switch schemes (remember: opting into Composition for a financial year via Form CMP-02 must be done before March 31 of the preceding year):

  1. Choose Composition (1%) ONLY IF: 100% of your sales are intra-state retail B2C, your output GST rate would be 12%–18% on MRP-locked goods, your input GST (ITC) is under 4% of turnover, and you have zero plans to sell on Amazon/Flipkart or to GST-registered B2B buyers.
  2. Choose Regular GST (QRMP) IF: You ship D2C parcels across state lines, sell to corporate or wholesale buyers who want tax invoices, spend heavily on 18% GST inputs (ads, packaging, SaaS, courier, marketplace fees), or expect to cross ₹1.5 crore within 18 months.
  3. Audit Your Supplier Bills First: Pull the last 90 days of purchase, Meta/Google Ads, and Shiprocket/Delhivery invoices. Add up the total GST column. If annualized input GST exceeds ₹1,20,000, Regular GST almost always beats Composition on total business economics.
  4. Update Your Store Copy & Quotes: If you move to Regular GST, clearly mention "GST Invoice Available for Input Tax Credit" on your product pages and Google Business Profile. You can draft clean, pixel-checked meta titles and GBP descriptions in CopyCraft so B2B buyers spot your ITC eligibility directly on Google Search.
  5. Keep Your Sales Register Clean: Whether you issue a Bill of Supply (Composition) or a Tax Invoice (Regular), export every bill into a clean CSV register using BillFold so your quarterly CMP-08 or GSTR-1 reconciliation takes 10 minutes instead of 2 days.

The bottom line is simple: Composition protects margins only for local, low-ITC, MRP-locked retail shops. For any growing Indian D2C brand or B2B supplier at ₹40 lakh turnover, Regular GST with full ₹2.88 lakh ITC recovery and pan-India shipping headroom is the stronger financial engine.
