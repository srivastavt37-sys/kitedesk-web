Partial COD & Advance Booking Token Accounting in India: GST Invoicing, Split UPI Receipts & Zero-Loss Dispatch

November 23, 2026 • 9 min read • 1751 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

If you sell made-to-order footwear, embroidered kurtis, custom phone cases, or engraved gifts in India, pure Cash on Delivery (COD) is a slow leak in your bank account. A buyer taps your Instagram ad at 11:15 PM, types a half-complete street address, picks COD, and forgets about the parcel three days later when the courier calls.

When that parcel comes back as Return to Origin (RTO), you do not just lose ₹135 in forward shipping and ₹135 in reverse shipping. On a custom ₹2,400 order, you also eat ₹80 in damaged corrugated packaging and ₹250 in custom work you cannot resell at full price. At 26% RTO across 100 monthly orders, those 26 failed deliveries quietly burn ₹15,600 in direct courier and prep losses before you even count ₹2,200 in COD handling fees and NDR calling hours.

Switching to Partial COD—collecting a ₹250 UPI booking token upfront and ₹2,150 on delivery—cuts RTO from 26% to 5% and saves ₹14,200 every month across 100 orders. The catch is that many Indian sellers break their GST books when they split one order into two payments. They either print a ₹2,150 invoice for the courier (under-reporting turnover by ₹250 per order) or forget to deduct the ₹250 advance on the customer slip and trigger a dispute at the doorstep.

1. The ₹2,400 Custom-Order Math: Pure COD vs. ₹250 Partial COD

Let us put real Indian rupee numbers on the table for a custom D2C studio shipping 100 orders a month at a ₹2,400 average order value (AOV). Assume each failed delivery costs ₹600 total: ₹135 forward courier fee, ₹135 reverse courier fee, ₹80 box and protective wrap write-off, and ₹250 in customisation or repacking loss.

Look closely at why Partial COD works so well on Tier-2 and Tier-3 buyers. Asking a first-time shopper in Surat, Jaipur, Indore, or Lucknow to pay ₹2,400 upfront feels risky to them if they have never bought from your store before. Asking for a ₹250 booking token on UPI—with ₹2,150 payable when the parcel reaches their gate—proves buyer intent without demanding full trust on day one.

2. The GST Invoicing Rule Most Sellers Get Wrong

Here is the single biggest accounting mistake Indian sellers make with Partial COD: they collect ₹250 via Google Pay or PhonePe on Tuesday, dispatch the parcel on Wednesday, and generate a tax invoice for ₹2,150 so the courier collects the right cash amount.

Do not do this. Under Indian GST law, the taxable value of supply is the full consideration for the goods—₹2,400, not ₹2,150. If you issue a ₹2,150 invoice for a ₹2,400 sale:

  • Your GSTR-1 outward supply under-reports ₹250 per order, which creates a ₹25,000 monthly mismatch across 100 orders when your CA reconciles bank UPI credits against GST filings.
  • If you are audited or apply for an MSME working-capital overdraft, your bank statement shows ₹2,40,000 collected while your invoices only total ₹2,15,000.
  • Your buyer receives a bill showing ₹2,150 and wonders where their ₹250 booking token went, or accuses your team of hiding the advance.

The clean, 100% compliant method is simple: always invoice the full ₹2,400 supply value (including GST), and show the ₹250 booking token as an Advance / Token Paid deduction below the Grand Total to arrive at a Balance Payable of ₹2,150.

Exact GST Breakdown for a ₹2,400 Custom Order (12% GST Inclusive)

Suppose you sell handcrafted footwear or custom apparel under HSN 6403 or HSN 6205 at a 12% GST-inclusive retail price of ₹2,400. Here is how the tax invoice splits cleanly:

  • Taxable Base Value: ₹2,400 ÷ 1.12 = ₹2,142.86
  • CGST (6%) + SGST (6%) for Intra-State (or 12% IGST for Inter-State): ₹128.57 + ₹128.57 = ₹257.14
  • Invoice Grand Total: ₹2,400.00 (reported in GSTR-1)
  • Less: Advance / Token Paid (via UPI): -₹250.00
  • Balance Payable on Delivery (COD / Doorstep UPI): ₹2,150.00

In BillFold, you can enter 250 directly into the Advance / Token Paid (₹) field. BillFold keeps the legal GST Grand Total at ₹2,400.00, displays Advance / Token Paid: -₹250.00 and Balance Payable: ₹2,150.00 cleanly below the total, and automatically locks the embedded upi://pay QR code and WhatsApp payment message to ₹2,150.00.

3. Step-by-Step Split UPI Receipt & Zero-Loss Dispatch Workflow

A Partial COD system only saves ₹14,200/month if your team executes it in under two minutes per order without messy spreadsheets. Follow this four-step daily dispatch loop:

Step 1: Collect the ₹250 Booking Token via a Pre-Filled WhatsApp Link

Right after a buyer places a COD order on your store or DMs you on Instagram, send a short, polite WhatsApp confirmation instead of calling them cold. Use TapPing to build a one-tap wa.me link with a pre-filled message that states the order number, the ₹250 token requirement, and your verified UPI ID.

Pair that with clear, trust-building storefront copy created in CopyCraft on your checkout page and Instagram bio—for example: "Custom orders ship after a ₹250 UPI booking token. Remaining ₹2,150 payable on delivery." When buyers see that rule before they hit order, 82% pay the token within 15 minutes.

Step 2: Record the Token Reference in Invoice Notes

The moment the ₹250 hits your bank account, note the last 6 digits of the UPI UTR number in your order sheet and paste it into the Payment Terms & Notes box in BillFold (for example: Advance ₹250 received via UPI UTR 632819. Balance ₹2,150 payable on delivery.). That single line ends 99% of doorstep confusion because the delivery rider and the customer both see the exact UTR proof printed on the A4 slip.

Step 3: Print the Slip and Lock the Doorstep QR to ₹2,150

Print the BillFold invoice and tape one copy inside the parcel (or in the outer AWB pouch). Because BillFold locks the upi://pay QR code to the Balance Payable (₹2,150.00) rather than the ₹2,400 Grand Total, the customer can scan the slip right in front of the courier or pay ₹2,150 cash—zero mental math, zero overpayment, and zero risk of the buyer claiming they already paid ₹250 twice.

Step 4: Set the Shipping Label COD Value to ₹2,150

On your courier aggregator dashboard (Shiprocket, Delhivery, or India Post), enter the declared product value as ₹2,400 for insurance and e-way/GST accuracy, and enter the COD Collectible Amount as ₹2,150. Never enter ₹2,400 in the COD collection box when ₹250 has already been paid, or your rider will demand ₹2,400 at the door and trigger an instant refusal.

4. Handling Cancellations, Refunds & Unclaimed Tokens Cleanly

What happens to the 5% of Partial COD orders that still fail delivery or cancel mid-production? Your GST treatment depends on whether you refund the token or forfeit it to cover custom work:

  • Scenario A — Buyer cancels before dispatch and you refund ₹250: Issue a Credit Note or Refund Voucher for ₹250 against the advance receipt, reverse the tax entry in your monthly summary, and pay back via the original UPI ID.
  • Scenario B — Custom work is already finished and buyer refuses delivery (5% RTO): State clearly on your checkout and WhatsApp confirmation that custom-order booking tokens are non-refundable once production starts. Retain the ₹250 token as forfeited cancellation charges (which offsets ₹250 of your ₹600 RTO cost per failed parcel) and issue a GST Credit Note for the uncollected ₹2,150 goods return so you only pay GST on the retained ₹250 service/forfeiture fee if applicable.

Keep your cancellation rule in plain English—two sentences maximum—on your product page and WhatsApp order message. Buyers respect clear, fair boundaries when they know why a custom studio asks for ₹250 upfront.

5. B2B Milestone Retainers Use the Exact Same Split Math

Partial COD is not just for ₹2,400 D2C parcels. Agencies, packaging printers, wholesale garment makers, and freelance designers face the same problem with 50% advance retainers. Suppose you invoice a retail client ₹47,200 (₹40,000 + 18% GST) for custom corrugated boxes and already collected ₹23,600 as a booking advance last week.

Using BillFold, you keep the taxable supply at ₹40,000 + ₹7,200 GST = ₹47,200, enter 23600 in Advance / Token Paid, and send the client a clean invoice showing Balance Payable: ₹23,600.00 with a locked UPI QR and a ready-to-send WhatsApp payment message. Your CA gets an exact ₹47,200 GST record, your client sees their prior ₹23,600 payment credited on paper, and you collect the remaining ₹23,600 in one scan.

6. Quick Pre-Dispatch Checklist for Zero-Loss Partial COD

  • Checkout & Bio Clarity: Update your Instagram bio and product description via CopyCraft so buyers expect the ₹250 booking token before ordering.
  • Unit Economics Check: Run your courier slab and packaging cost through MarginMint to confirm your token covers at least 40% of a round-trip RTO loss.
  • 15-Minute WhatsApp Nudge: Send a pre-filled TapPing UPI token request within 15 minutes of every COD order before starting custom prep.
  • Full-Value GST + Split Balance Slip: Generate your ₹2,400 GST invoice in BillFold with 250 in Advance Paid so GSTR-1 stays accurate and the doorstep QR locks to ₹2,150.
  • AWB Double-Check: Confirm Declared Value = ₹2,400 and COD Collectible = ₹2,150 before handing the parcel to your pickup rider.
