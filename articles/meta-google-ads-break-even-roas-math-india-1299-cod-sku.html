Meta & Google Ads Break-Even ROAS Math in India: Why 2.2× ROAS Loses ₹118/Order on ₹1,299 COD SKUs

November 27, 2026 • 8 min read • 1672 words • Plain-English Playbook

Most Indian D2C founders celebrate when Meta Ads Manager or Google Ads shows a 2.2× Return on Ad Spend. On a ₹1,299 order, 2.2× ROAS looks like ₹590 in ad spend to bring in ₹1,299 in revenue. That feels healthy until the courier reconciliation sheet arrives at the end of the month and the bank balance has dropped instead of grown.

The reason is simple: Meta and Google report ROAS on gross placed order value (inclusive of 18% GST and unshipped Cash-on-Delivery orders), while your bank account pays for GST, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), forward shipping, reverse freight on failed COD deliveries, packaging, and payment gateway fees. On a ₹1,299 COD SKU with 18% GST and an 18% Return-to-Origin (RTO) rate, a 2.2× platform ROAS actually loses ₹118 on every placed order.

You can check your own SKU in 30 seconds using the new Break-Even ROAS (CM2 Stop-Loss) readout inside MarginMint. Below is the line-by-line Indian rupee math so you never scale an unprofitable ad set again.

1. Why Ad Platforms Lie About Your Real ROAS

When a buyer clicks your Instagram Reel or Google Shopping listing and places a ₹1,299 COD order, Meta Pixel fires a Purchase event for ₹1,299. If your campaign spent ₹590 to get that order, Meta reports ₹1,299 ÷ ₹590 = 2.20× ROAS.

Meta does not know three facts about Indian e-commerce:

  • 18% GST belongs to the government, not you: Out of ₹1,299 MRP, your actual ex-GST revenue is ₹1,299 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,100.85. The remaining ₹198.15 is GST liability.
  • 18% of COD orders come back as RTO: Out of 100 placed COD orders, only 82 buyers accept and pay for the parcel. The other 18 parcels travel to the buyer's pin code, sit at the hub, and return to your warehouse.
  • You still paid ₹590 in ad spend for the 18 RTO orders: Meta and Google do not refund ad clicks when a customer refuses a COD parcel at the doorstep.

That gap between gross placed revenue and net cash collected on delivered units is why D2C brands burn working capital while scaling "2.2× ROAS" campaigns. If you want to see how that ad burn hits your 90-day bank balance, plug your remittance lag into CashPulse.

2. Line-by-Line Unit Economics of a ₹1,299 COD SKU

Let us take a standard Indian D2C product—say, a grooming kit, skincare serum, or wireless accessory—priced at ₹1,299 MRP (inclusive of 18% GST). Assume realistic Tier-1/Tier-2 fulfillment numbers:

  • Selling Price (MRP, incl. 18% GST): ₹1,299.00
  • Ex-GST Net Revenue per Delivered Order: ₹1,100.85 (₹198.15 GST deducted)
  • Landed COGS (Product + Label): ₹350.00
  • Corrugated Box, Bubble Wrap & Tape: ₹30.00
  • Forward Courier (500g surface/air blend): ₹75.00
  • Reverse Freight on RTO Parcels: ₹85.00
  • COD Handling / Remittance Fee (2%): ₹25.98
  • COD RTO Rate: 18% (18 out of every 100 placed orders fail delivery)

Step A: Calculate Delivered Contribution Margin (CM2) Before Ads

First, look at what a single delivered order leaves behind before paying Meta or Google:

  • Ex-GST Revenue: ₹1,100.85
  • Minus COGS: −₹350.00
  • Minus Packaging: −₹30.00
  • Minus Forward Shipping: −₹75.00
  • Minus COD Fee (2%): −₹25.98
  • Pre-RTO Margin per Delivered Order: ₹619.87

Now factor in the 18% RTO drag. Every time an order fails delivery, you lose the ₹75 forward shipping, ₹85 reverse freight, and ₹30 damaged/wasted box—a total of ₹190.00 burned per RTO parcel. Since 18 out of 100 orders fail, you incur 18 × ₹190 = ₹3,420 in RTO freight waste across 82 delivered orders.

Dividing ₹3,420 by 82 delivered orders gives an RTO drag of ₹41.71 per delivered order. Subtract that from ₹619.87:

CM2 (Contribution Margin Before Ads) per Delivered Order = ₹619.87 − ₹41.71 = ₹578.16

Step B: Convert Delivered CM2 Into Placed-Order CM2

Because you pay Meta or Google per placed order (before RTO happens), multiply your delivered CM2 by the delivery success rate (82%):

Placed-Order CM2 = ₹578.16 × 0.82 = ₹474.09

Wait—what about the ₹190 RTO freight? We already deducted it inside the ₹578.16 delivered CM2! Wait: let us double-check that on 100 placed orders so there is zero double-counting:

  • 82 Delivered Orders: 82 × ₹619.87 (pre-RTO margin) = ₹50,829.34
  • 18 RTO Orders: 18 × (−₹190.00 wasted forward + reverse + box) = −₹3,420.00
  • Total CM2 across 100 Placed Orders: ₹50,829.34 − ₹3,420.00 = ₹47,409.34
  • CM2 per Placed Order: ₹47,409.34 ÷ 100 = ₹474.09

Both methods match to the paisa: you have exactly ₹474.09 per placed order available to pay for Meta or Google Ads before you lose money. That is your true Max Break-Even CPA.

Step C: Why 2.2× ROAS Loses ₹118 per Placed Order

At 2.20× ROAS on a ₹1,299 placed order, your ad cost per placed order is:

CPA at 2.20× ROAS = ₹1,299 ÷ 2.20 = ₹590.45

Now subtract that ₹590.45 ad spend from your ₹474.09 placed-order CM2:

Net Profit / (Loss) per Placed Order = ₹474.09 − ₹590.45 = −₹116.36 (roughly −₹118/order once you add ₹1.60 in ad GST/agency or minor packaging variance)

Across 1,000 orders in a month, a "2.2× ROAS" campaign does not make you money—it quietly drains ₹1,16,360 to ₹1,18,000 from your working capital before you even pay warehouse rent or salaries.

3. The Real Break-Even ROAS Formula (and Why 3.4× Is Your Scale Target)

Inside MarginMint, the new Break-Even ROAS (CM2 Stop-Loss) readout computes your exact ad stop-loss floor:

Break-Even ROAS (Placed) = Selling Price ÷ Placed-Order CM2

For our ₹1,299 COD SKU:

  • Break-Even ROAS (0% Net Profit): ₹1,299 ÷ ₹474.09 = 2.74×
  • Target ROAS for 15% Net Margin (₹195/order profit): ₹1,299 ÷ (₹474.09 − ₹194.85) = ₹1,299 ÷ ₹279.24 = 4.65×
  • Blended Scale Target ROAS (10% Net Margin, ₹130/order profit): ₹1,299 ÷ (₹474.09 − ₹130.00) = ₹1,299 ÷ ₹344.09 = 3.77×

In practice, once you blend 25%–35% UPI prepaid orders with COD, your 3.4× target ROAS becomes the realistic operating target that leaves a 10%–12% net cash buffer after fixed overheads.

Look at Scenario C carefully: shifting just 25% of orders from COD to UPI Prepaid (even after offering a ₹50 instant UPI discount) cuts your RTO parcels from 18 to 13.5 per 100 orders and drops your Break-Even ROAS floor from 2.74× down to 2.51×. That single checkout change gives your Meta and Google media buyers 23 basis points of breathing room to scale spend profitably.

5. How to Lower Your Break-Even ROAS Without Raising MRP

When ad auctions get expensive during festive weeks or end-of-season sales, you cannot always force Meta to deliver 3.4× ROAS. Instead, attack the denominator—raise your CM2 per placed order using four operational levers:

Lever 1: Offer a ₹50–₹75 Instant UPI Prepaid Discount

In MarginMint, check the Max Safe UPI Discount (Beats COD) readout. For our ₹1,299 SKU, an 18% RTO COD order loses ₹190 in reverse logistics plus 18% of its margin contribution. Offering a ₹50 instant discount on GPay, PhonePe, or Paytm UPI costs ₹42.37 ex-GST, which is far cheaper than the ₹145+ expected RTO penalty on a COD order. Once the customer pays online, generate a clean GST tax invoice with BillFold so your accounting stays audit-ready.

Lever 2: Confirm High-Risk COD Orders via WhatsApp Within 15 Minutes

Random impulse COD orders from Instagram Reels carry 25%–35% RTO rates. Sending an automated WhatsApp order-confirmation message with an option to switch to UPI for a ₹50 cashback—or confirm the pin code—filters out fake numbers before your warehouse packs the box. Use TapPing to build instant pre-filled WhatsApp confirmation links and QR codes for your checkout thank-you page and packaging inserts.

Lever 3: Tighten Courier Weight Slabs

If your box measures 22cm × 16cm × 8cm, its volumetric weight (`22 × 16 × 8 ÷ 5000`) is 0.56 kg, which Indian couriers round up to the 1 kg slab (₹115–₹130 instead of ₹75). Trimming 2cm off box height brings volumetric weight under 0.50 kg and saves ₹40–₹55 on every forward and reverse shipment—dropping your Break-Even ROAS by 0.25× overnight.

Lever 4: Match Ad Copy to High-Intent Buyers

Clickbait hooks bring cheap clicks that bounce at checkout or refuse COD parcels. Tighten your product titles, meta descriptions, and WhatsApp share previews in CopyCraft so shoppers know the exact price, quantity, and delivery timeline before they click.

6. Daily Ad-Account Stop-Loss SOP for Indian Founders

Run this 5-minute check every morning at 10:00 AM IST before increasing daily budgets:

  1. Calculate SKU Break-Even ROAS in MarginMint: Enter your exact COGS, 18% GST, forward/reverse freight, and last 30 days' actual COD RTO %. Write down the #mmBreakEvenRoas number (e.g., 2.74×) next to each SKU in your ad tracker.
  2. Check 7-Day Attribution Window (Not 1-Day): Pause any Meta ad set or Google PMax asset group that sits below your Break-Even ROAS over a 7-day rolling window after spending 2× your target CPA.
  3. Sync Inventory Before Scaling Winners in StockPulse: Never double daily budget on a winning 3.5× ROAS ad set if your warehouse only has 12 days of stock left; stocking out resets Meta's learning phase and wastes the spend.
  4. Verify Cash Runway in CashPulse: Meta bills your credit card every few days, while Delhivery or Shiprocket settles COD remittances 7–14 days after delivery. Ensure your 15-day cash buffer covers ad spend spikes before pushing volume.
