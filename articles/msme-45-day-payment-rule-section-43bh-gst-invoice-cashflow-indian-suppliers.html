MSME 45-Day Payment Rule (Section 43B(h)) & GST Invoice Cashflow Playbook for Indian Suppliers

November 28, 2026 • 9 min read • 1794 words • Plain-English Playbook

If you supply packaging, apparel, corporate gifting, or raw ingredients to larger Indian companies, your biggest cashflow leak is rarely a bad product. It is a buyer sitting on a ₹4,50,000 invoice for 75 or 90 days while you still have to pay 18% GST—₹81,000 in hard cash—to the government by the 20th of the next month.

Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act changed the leverage in that negotiation. When a buyer fails to pay a registered Micro or Small enterprise within the statutory 15-day or 45-day window, the buyer loses the right to deduct that expense in that financial year. That single rule turns a polite "accounts team will process next week" excuse into a ₹1,13,256 tax penalty on the buyer's own balance sheet.

Most Indian suppliers still fail to collect on time because their invoices lack Udyam registration numbers, written credit-period clauses, and a structured WhatsApp follow-up trail. This playbook breaks down the exact Section 43B(h) rupee math, the GST cashflow trap, and the 4-step collection system you can run straight from BillFold and CashPulse.

1. What Section 43B(h) Actually Says (Without Legal Jargon)

Section 43B(h) links the Income Tax Act directly to Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006. It applies when a buyer purchases goods or services from a supplier registered on the Udyam portal as a Micro or Small enterprise.

  • No written credit agreement: The buyer must pay within 15 days from the date of acceptance (or deemed acceptance) of the goods or services.
  • Written credit agreement exists: You and the buyer can agree in writing to a longer credit period, but the law caps it at a maximum of 45 days. Even if a corporate PO says "Net 60" or "Net 90", Section 43B(h) overrides that clause at 45 days.
  • Who is covered: Only Micro (turnover up to ₹5 crore, plant/machinery investment up to ₹1 crore) and Small (turnover up to ₹50 crore, investment up to ₹10 crore) enterprises with an active Udyam Registration Certificate. Medium enterprises and pure traders (unless registered under retail/wholesale Udyam with specific caveats—note that wholesale/retail traders registered only for Priority Sector Lending are excluded from Section 43B(h) delayed-payment protection) do not get this protection.

The penalty is simple: if the invoice is still unpaid beyond the 15-day or 45-day limit at the end of the financial year (March 31), the buyer cannot claim that purchase as a business expense in that year. They can only deduct it in the year they actually pay you.

2. The ₹4,50,000 Invoice Math: Why Buyers Care About March 31

Numbers settle arguments faster than legal threats. Suppose your studio in Jaipur supplies ₹4,50,000 worth of custom rigid boxes (plus 18% GST = ₹81,000, Grand Total ₹5,31,000) to a D2C skincare company in Mumbai on February 10, 2027.

Your invoice states a 30-day credit term, making the due date March 12, 2027. The 45-day statutory ceiling ends on March 27, 2027. Suppose the buyer's finance team drags their feet and pays you on April 14, 2027 instead of March 27.

Line Item Paid by March 27, 2027 (On Time) Paid on April 14, 2027 (Section 43B(h) Triggered)
Taxable Supply Value ₹4,50,000 ₹4,50,000
GST Charged (18% IGST) ₹81,000 ₹81,000
Expense Deductible in FY 2026-27 ₹4,50,000 ₹0 (Disallowed u/s 43B(h))
Buyer Taxable Profit Increase ₹0 +₹4,50,000
Extra Tax Buyer Pays (at 25.168% Corporate Rate) ₹0 ₹1,13,256
MSMED Compound Interest Exposure (3× RBI Bank Rate) ₹0 ~₹15,000+ if disputed at Samadhaan

Think about that. By delaying your ₹4,50,000 invoice by just 18 days past March 27, the buyer's CFO ends up paying ₹1,13,256 in extra income tax upfront in FY 2026-27. If the buyer is a partnership firm or LLP taxed at 31.2%, that tax hit jumps to ₹1,40,400. When you explain this politely in a reminder on Day 30, you are not nagging—you are saving their CA from an audit disallowance.

3. The Supplier's GST Cashflow Trap (Why 60-Day Credit Kills Working Capital)

While the buyer enjoys free credit for 60 or 90 days, your business faces a brutal timing mismatch under Indian GST rules:

  1. Output GST is due on accrual, not cash receipt: For that February 10 invoice, you must report ₹81,000 of IGST in GSTR-1 by March 11 and pay it in GSTR-3B by March 20—even if the buyer has not paid you a single rupee.
  2. Raw material suppliers demand advance or 7-day payment: Your duplex board mill and ink vendor do not wait 75 days. If your material + labour cost on that ₹4,50,000 job is ₹3,15,000, you have already spent ₹3,15,000 + ₹81,000 GST = ₹3,96,000 out of pocket before March 20.
  3. Overdraft interest eats your net margin: Financing ₹3,96,000 on an 11.5% CC/OD limit for 60 extra days costs ₹7,486 in bank interest. On a 15% net margin job (₹67,500 profit), a 60-day delay plus bank interest and follow-up time cuts your real return by more than 11%.

4. Four Things Every B2B GST Invoice Must Carry

A buyer's auditor can only flag Section 43B(h) automatically if your invoice makes your Micro/Small status unmistakable. Never send a generic PDF without these four fields:

  • Exact Udyam Registration Number (URN): Print UDYAM-XX-00-0000000 and category (e.g., Micro Enterprise - Manufacturing) right inside the invoice header or notes block.
  • Explicit Credit Period Clause: State Payment Terms: 30 Days from Invoice Date (MSME Section 43B(h) Max 45 Days). Without a written term, the default is 15 days, which often triggers friction; 30 days gives both teams a clear 15-day buffer before the 45-day statutory cliff.
  • Acceptance / Delivery Date Proof: Record the LR/AWB number or signed delivery challan date. Under MSMED Section 15, the 15/45-day clock starts from the day of actual delivery if the buyer raises no written objection within 15 days.
  • Instant UPI + Bank Remittance Block: Even for ₹50,000–₹1,00,000 invoices, embedding a dynamic upi://pay QR code via BillFold removes "wait for NEFT beneficiary cooling period" delays for smaller B2B orders, while displaying your NEFT/RTGS details for larger ₹2,00,000+ transfers.

6. Pricing Early-Payment Discounts Without Destroying Margin

What if a large retail chain insists on 60-day or 90-day credit, or asks for a 2% cash discount (CD) to pay in 7 days? Run the math before saying yes.

Suppose your ₹4,50,000 taxable order carries a 22% gross margin (₹99,000). Giving a 2% cash discount on the taxable value costs you ₹9,000. Compare that ₹9,000 against what 60 days of delayed cash actually costs you:

  • Bank OD cost saved (53 days earlier at 12% p.a. on ₹5,31,000): ₹9,252 saved.
  • Zero bad-debt or Samadhaan follow-up risk: ₹0 collection overhead.
  • Ability to turn that ₹3,15,000 working capital one extra time: If you reinvest that cash into another 22% margin batch, you earn another ₹99,000 gross profit.

In short, a 1.5% to 2.0% early-payment discount for Day 7 NEFT/UPI settlement is almost always cheaper than waiting 60+ days—provided your base gross margin is above 18%. Verify your exact unit economics after discounts and GST using MarginMint.

7. Quick Pre-Year-End Audit Checklist (Every February & March)

  1. Download your Sales Register: Export your saved invoices as CSV from BillFold and filter every B2B invoice older than 25 days.
  2. Verify buyer Udyam requests: Corporate buyers now send annual MSME declaration emails in February. Reply within 48 hours with your Udyam certificate PDF so your vendor code is tagged MSME-Micro or MSME-Small in their ERP.
  3. Separate disputed vs undisputed bills: Under MSMED Section 15, if a buyer has a genuine quality dispute, they must raise it in writing within 15 days of delivery. Silent delays are legally deemed accepted.
  4. Clear stale local test bills: Keep your browser register clean with Clear Register in BillFold after exporting your monthly CA backup JSON/CSV so your active follow-up list only shows real unpaid invoices.

Cashflow discipline is not about having the loudest collection calls. It is about putting your Udyam number on every BillFold invoice, tracking your receivable buffer in CashPulse, and firing a clear, polite Day 20 and Day 40 reminder before a ₹4,50,000 invoice turns into a working-capital bottleneck.
