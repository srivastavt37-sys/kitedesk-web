QRMP Scheme vs Monthly GST Filing Math for Indian Sellers Under ₹5 Crore: IFF Uploads, PMT-06 & Cash-Flow Timing

November 23, 2026 • 10 min read • 1718 words • Plain-English Playbook
WATCH OUR ORIGINAL AI-NARRATED WALKTHROUGH

If your Indian D2C brand, wholesale unit, or service agency does under ₹5 crore in annual aggregate turnover, choosing between monthly GST returns and the Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme is not a paperwork preference. It is a direct cash-flow and compliance-cost decision. Get it wrong, and you either pay ₹3,000–₹5,000 every month to a CA for 12 full GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B cycles, or you delay your B2B buyers' Input Tax Credit (ITC) by two months and watch them hold back your invoice payments.

Most Indian sellers avoid QRMP because they hear one half-truth: "If I file quarterly, my B2B buyers will not see their GST credit in GSTR-2B every month." That was true before the Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF) existed. Today, you can stay on QRMP, upload only your B2B invoices via IFF in Month 1 and Month 2, pay tax through a simple PMT-06 challan, and file one consolidated GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B at the end of the quarter. Let us run the exact ₹18,00,000 quarterly turnover math to see how much time, CA fees, and working capital you actually save.

1. The ₹18,00,000 Quarterly Turnover Baseline

Suppose you run a Surat-based apparel and packaging supply business with ₹72,00,000 in annual turnover, which puts you comfortably under the ₹5 crore QRMP eligibility ceiling. In Q3 (October–December), your total taxable sales equal ₹18,00,000 at an average 12% GST slab (₹2,16,000 gross output GST before ITC).

Here is how that ₹18,00,000 splits across channels every month:

  • Monthly B2B Wholesale Orders: ₹2,50,000 per month across 14 GST-registered retail buyers (₹7,50,000 per quarter). Output GST at 12% = ₹30,000/month.
  • Monthly B2C D2C & Marketplace Orders: ₹3,50,000 per month across 280 retail orders (₹10,50,000 per quarter). Output GST at 12% = ₹42,000/month.
  • Eligible Monthly Purchase ITC: ₹1,90,000 in raw fabric, corrugated boxes, and courier bills at 12%–18% average GST = ₹26,000/month in input credit (₹78,000/quarter).

Your net cash GST liability after ITC is ₹72,000 output minus ₹26,000 input = ₹46,000 per month (₹1,38,000 for the quarter). Both monthly filing and QRMP collect the exact same ₹1,38,000 in tax over 90 days. What changes completely is how many filings you prepare, when your buyers get their GSTR-2B credit, and how much you spend on compliance.

2. Monthly Filing vs QRMP + IFF: Side-by-Side Overhead Comparison

When a business under ₹5 crore stays on regular monthly filing, it must reconcile and file 24 full returns per year (12 GSTR-1 + 12 GSTR-3B). Under QRMP with IFF, you file 4 quarterly GSTR-1 returns, 4 quarterly GSTR-3B returns, and up to 8 lightweight IFF uploads containing only B2B invoices (no B2C state-wise HSN summaries in Months 1 and 2).

Notice the 65% drop in filing overhead. Over a full financial year, saving ₹6,900 per quarter keeps ₹27,600 in cash inside your business and frees up 35+ hours of founder time that would otherwise go into matching B2C marketplace state codes every single month.

3. How IFF Keeps Your B2B Buyers' GSTR-2B Active Every Month

The single biggest objection from corporate or wholesale buyers is: "We only buy from monthly filers because we need our ITC reflected on the 14th of next month." With IFF, that objection disappears if you follow the 13th-of-the-month cutoff.

Here is the exact timeline for our ₹18,00,000 quarterly seller in Q3:

  • October (Month 1): You raise 14 B2B invoices worth ₹2,50,000 + ₹30,000 GST using BillFold with each buyer's 15-digit GSTIN verified and HSN codes locked. Between November 1 and November 13, you upload only those 14 B2B invoices to the GST portal via IFF. Your 280 B2C orders stay in your local register. On November 14, your B2B buyers' GSTR-2B auto-populates with the full ₹30,000 ITC—identical to a monthly filer.
  • November (Month 2): You raise 14 B2B invoices worth ₹2,50,000 + ₹30,000 GST. Between December 1 and December 13, you upload those 14 B2B invoices via IFF. Your buyers get their ₹30,000 ITC on December 14 without waiting for January.
  • December (Month 3): There is no IFF in Month 3. Instead, by January 13, you file your quarterly GSTR-1 containing December's 14 B2B invoices plus the consolidated Q3 B2C state-wise summary (₹10,50,000) and HSN table. Invoices already uploaded via October and November IFF do not need to be re-entered.

One strict rule applies to IFF: the monthly upload cap is ₹50,00,000 in taxable B2B value. Since our example seller does ₹2,50,000/month in B2B sales (and even a ₹1.5 crore/year B2B seller averages ₹12.5 lakh/month), 99% of sellers under ₹5 crore never hit that ₹50 lakh monthly IFF ceiling.

4. PMT-06 Monthly Tax Payment: Fixed Sum Method vs Self-Assessment

Under QRMP, you still deposit tax every month by the 25th of the following month using Form PMT-06 for Month 1 and Month 2, and settle the final balance with GSTR-3B in Month 3 (by the 22nd or 24th depending on your state). You get two legal ways to calculate that PMT-06 challan:

Option A: Fixed Sum Method (35% Challan)

If your previous quarter's cash GST payout was ₹1,38,000, the GST portal auto-generates a PMT-06 challan for 35% of that amount: ₹1,38,000 × 35% = ₹48,300 for October and ₹48,300 for November. At the end of December, your total quarterly liability is ₹1,38,000, and you have already paid ₹96,600, so your Month 3 GSTR-3B balance is ₹41,400.

Why founders like Option A: Zero interest risk. Even if your November sales spike to ₹9,00,000 because of Diwali demand, paying the auto-generated 35% challan by the 25th shields you from 18% annual late-payment interest under Section 50, provided you file your quarterly GSTR-3B on time.

Option B: Self-Assessment Method (Actual Liability)

If your business is seasonal and October was slow—say ₹1,50,000 in sales with ₹18,000 output GST and ₹15,000 in purchase ITC—paying a fixed ₹48,300 challan locks up ₹45,300 of your working capital for two months. Under Self-Assessment, you pay only your actual net cash liability (₹3,000) via PMT-06 by November 25.

The catch with Option B: If your actual calculation misses a supplier invoice that did not appear in GSTR-2B, you owe 18% p.a. interest on the shortfall. Always check your vendor GSTR-2B status before choosing Self-Assessment.

5. Clean Invoice Data Stops 90% of IFF Rejections

IFF uploads fail or get delayed for three mundane reasons: an invalid buyer GSTIN, a missing Place of Supply state code, or a rounding mismatch between unit price, tax rate, and invoice total. Fixing 14 B2B invoices manually on the 12th of the month wipes out the time QRMP was supposed to save.

Before you send any B2B bill, standardize your invoice template in BillFold:

  • Lock CGST/SGST vs IGST: Intra-state buyers get split CGST + SGST; inter-state buyers get IGST. One wrong tax mode forces a credit note and a manual IFF amendment next month.
  • Include MSME Section 43B(h) Terms: Under Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act, corporate buyers lose tax deductions if they delay payments to registered micro and small enterprises beyond 15 days (or 45 days with a written agreement). Adding your MSME registration line directly inside the invoice notes nudges accounts payable teams to clear your ₹2,50,000 B2B batch before the 25th PMT-06 deadline.
  • Verify Unit Margin After Tax: Before quoting a wholesale discount to win a B2B order, run the landed cost, GST slab, and courier fee through MarginMint so a 5% trade discount does not quietly erase your net profit.

6. Pairing GST Timing With WhatsApp Payment Nudges

Cash-flow timing under QRMP works best when your B2B receivables land before your PMT-06 challan due date on the 25th. If you upload your October IFF invoices by November 10 instead of waiting until the 13th, your buyers see their ITC early—which gives you a legitimate reason to follow up for payment on November 15.

Instead of writing awkward payment reminders from scratch, generate a clean one-tap UPI payment link or pre-filled WhatsApp follow-up message in TapPing referencing the exact invoice number and due date. And if you are pitching new wholesale accounts who ask why they should switch suppliers, use CopyCraft to draft crisp catalog descriptions and GST-ready quote summaries that emphasize reliable monthly IFF compliance.

7. When You Should NOT Use QRMP

QRMP is not for every seller under ₹5 crore. Stick to regular monthly filing if:

  • Your Monthly B2B Sales Exceed ₹50,00,000: IFF caps B2B uploads at ₹50 lakh per month. If you cross that in Month 1 or Month 2, the excess invoices cannot be uploaded until Month 3's GSTR-1, delaying your buyer's ITC.
  • You Claim Monthly Export Refunds (LUT / IGST): Exporters who depend on fast monthly GST refund cycles usually get cleaner processing on monthly GSTR-1/3B schedules.
  • Your Purchase ITC Exceeds Output Tax Every Month: If you are constantly in an inverted duty structure or accumulating credit, monthly GSTR-3B reconciliation helps you track refund eligibility without waiting 90 days.

8. The 15-Minute Monthly QRMP Checklist

If your turnover is under ₹5 crore and your B2B volume stays under ₹50 lakh/month, run this exact 3-step routine every month:

  1. By the 10th: Export all B2B tax invoices raised in the previous month from BillFold and verify every buyer GSTIN and HSN code.
  2. By the 13th: Upload only B2B invoices to IFF (Month 1 and Month 2) or file full GSTR-1 (Month 3). Confirm your buyers receive their GSTR-2B reflection on the 14th.
  3. By the 25th: Pay your PMT-06 challan using the Fixed Sum 35% option (for zero interest risk) or Self-Assessment (if sales dipped sharply), using receivables collected via TapPing.

For a ₹18,00,000-a-quarter seller, switching from monthly returns to QRMP + IFF keeps buyer ITC 100% intact, cuts filing hours by 65%, and saves ₹27,600 a year in routine compliance fees.
